Internet personality ‘Mr Ho’ dies at 79, just a week after receiving jail term
Source says Ho Huen, whose relationship with 45-year-old Ye Xiuding thrust couple into public spotlight, died in public hospital
A Hong Kong man known on the internet as “Mr Ho” has died, a week after receiving a two-month prison sentence for stabbing his wife.
A source told the South China Morning Post on Tuesday that Ho Huen, 79, had died in a public hospital a day earlier.
Ronson Chan Ron-sing, former chairman of the Hong Kong Journalists Association, also said on social media that Ho had died, citing people close to the deceased.
“He could not survive without being able to eat,” said Chan, who earlier revealed that he spent his five-day jail term in the same cell with Ho for obstructing public officers.
A week earlier, the District Court sentenced Ho to two months in prison for wounding his 45-year-old wife, Ye Xiuding, also known as “Mrs Ho”. He had learned that she had intended to leave the public rental flat they shared for mainland China on May 30, 2025.
Ho’s sentencing was announced during his absence as he was hospitalised for an undisclosed medical condition, with Justice Stanley Chan Kwong-chi deciding that the case should be concluded as early as possible.

Hong Kong steps up support as premium taxi fleets face driver shortage
More than 2,000 fleet taxis currently in service but number still falls short of government’s target of 3,500
A top Hong Kong transport official has pledged to step up efforts to help the city’s premium taxi fleets recruit drivers, conceding that the number of vehicles on the road remains about one-third below target a year after the scheme was launched.
As the five taxi fleets offering premium service marked a year of operations in July, the government said on Tuesday that more than 2,000 fleet taxis and about 2,400 drivers were currently in service.
As of the end of May, the five fleets had completed about 4.2 million trips in total.
However, the number of fleet taxis still falls short of the government’s target of 3,500.
The five licensed operators are SynCab, Big Boss Taxi, Amigo, Big Bee Taxi and Joie.
Commissioner for Transport Tse Wing-yee said the government had been actively promoting their development, but acknowledged that operators must “step up their efforts” in recruitment to attract more new blood to the industry.
“We have specific requirements regarding taxi fleets. I believe they will continue to improve,” she told a radio programme. “The number of fleet taxis has been steadily increasing, with individual fleets already reaching 85 per cent of their committed capacity.”

Museum goes viral for merchandise featuring bad Google review
- The New Bedford Whaling Museum has successfully turned a negative 2020 Google review, labelling it the “worst aquarium ever”, into a highly popular merchandise line.
- The merchandise, which features the one-star review on the back and a whale skeleton on the front, has garnered global attention and significantly boosted sales.
- Following a segment on CBS Evening News, the museum received 738 online orders for the “worst aquarium” items in under 24 hours, leading to a 250 per cent increase in weekly sales.
- Emily Roznoy, the museum's visitor experience manager, designed the merchandise after hearing staff jokes about the review and seeing a similar concept used by a restaurant.
- The museum, which recently installed a small aquarium exhibit, sees the witty merchandise as a way to connect with a broader audience and showcase its engaging, community-focused approach.
