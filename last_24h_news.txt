Premier League champions Arsenal announced Saturday the signing of Brazilian midfielder Bruno Guimaraes from Newcastle United on a long-term contract.
“We are delighted to announce that Brazil international Bruno Guimaraes has joined us from Newcastle United on a long-term contract,” Arsenal said in a statement.
The 28-year-old signed a four-year contract with an option for an additional season, while British media reported that the transfer was worth about $101.16 million.
“Bruno joins us having established himself as one of the most influential players in the Premier League,” the north London club said.
Arsenal Sporting Director Andrea Berta praised the midfielder’s versatility and said his arrival would strengthen manager Mikel Arteta’s squad.
“As we have all seen, Bruno can play as a defensive midfielder and as a box-to-box player. He combines quality with quantity, and has provided goals and assists for his team every year,” Berta said.
“Bruno will allow Mikel to further develop our playing style and will also increase our internal competitiveness, which is essential for maintaining the standards required when we are aiming to win major trophies.”
Guimaraes joined Newcastle from French club Olympique Lyonnais in January 2022 and made 195 appearances across all competitions, scoring 31 goals.
He captained Newcastle to the League Cup title in 2025, helping the club secure its first major domestic trophy in 70 years.
His departure represents another setback for Newcastle following the exits of Anthony Gordon and Sandro Tonali, as well as manager Eddie Howe, who stepped down and was replaced by German coach Matthias Jaissle.

Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar’s ruling Tisza party said on Saturday that it had nominated former Supreme Court President András Baka to become the country’s next president.
Baka, who worked as a judge on the European Court of Human Rights before being elected to head the Supreme Court, was removed from his post by the Fidesz government in 2011 — a move the ECHRruled was unlawful.
“Throughout his career, Dr. András Baka has considered the principle of the separation of powers to be of paramount importance and has consistently stood up for the rule of law and judicial independence,” Tisza said in a Facebook post.

He is “the strongest guarantee that the office of President of the Republic will regain its true dignity and its ability to unite the nation and serve as a counterbalance to the ruling majority,” it added.
Parliament is set to vote on his candidacy next Tuesday. Tisza, which won a general election in the spring, enjoys a two-thirds majority in the chamber, making Baka’s election to the largely representative post a formality.
The nomination comes after former President Tamás Sulyok signed a constitutional amendment that removed him from office last month, bowing to months of public pressure from Magyar. The new prime minister had called for Sulyok’s resignation following Tisza’s landslide election victory that ended Viktor Orbán’s 16-year rule.
Magyar’s first choice for president, chess grandmaster Judit Polgár, declined the nomination, saying the job would be too stressful.

Long-range Ukrainian drones have hit two oil refineries in Russia's Krasnodar Krai and Samara Oblast, the General Staff said on Aug. 8.
In Krasnodar Krai, in southern Russia, the Ilsky Oil Refinery sustained "a direct hit," sparking a fire on the plant's grounds, according to the General Staff. It added that the refinery has six processing units with a total capacity of about 6.6 million metric tons of crude oil per year and that its products are used to support the Russian military.
Over 1,000 kilometers northeast in Samara Oblast, the Syrzan Oil Refinery had also sustained "a direct hit," with the General Staff reporting a fire at the facility.
The refinery belongs to state-controlled oil company Rosneft and produces over 20 types of petroleum products, from motor fuels to road bitumen, according to the General Staff. It has a design capacity of about 8.5 million metric tons of crude oil annually, it added.
"The facility is one of the key sites in the Samara Region's oil refining industry and supplies fuel, in particular, to meet the needs of the Russian armed forces," the General Staff said in its Telegram statement.
The Russian Defense Ministry has not commented on the General Staff's claim. Moscow claimed it had downed 83 Ukrainian drones overnight, but did not name Krasnodar Krai or Samara Oblast among the regions over which the drones were intercepted.
The attacks come as Ukraine ramps up its long-range strikes on occupied Ukrainian territories and deep inside Russia, targeting oil refineries, airfields, vessels belonging to Russia's shadow fleet, energy infrastructure, and military-industrial facilities.
Outmanned and outgunned on the front, Ukraine is trying to slowly grind down Russia's combat capability from afar.
Ukraine's recent strikes have targeted some of the biggest oil refineries in Russia, seeking to further exacerbate the country's fuel crisis.
Russia has tapped into its fuel reserves, with stocks falling by about 4% compared to the previous year, Russian President Vladimir Putin claimed in late June at a meeting with leaders of the Russian energy industry.
