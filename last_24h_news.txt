Some 20 dogs competed in the 10th annual World Dog Surfing Championships at Linda Mar Beach in California last Saturday.
The sport was first conceived in 2006 by K. Reed, author of "The Dog's Guide to Surfing."
In the years since, the sport evolved into what it is today, and has inspired similar events across the world, including many in California, Florida, Australia, and the United Kingdom.
This year’s overall winner was five-year-old Labrador retriever Rippin Rosie, followed closely by Iza, a French bulldog from Pleasanton, and Bradley “Shortboard” Cooper, a mixed-breed from Long Beach.
Additionally, Rosie - alongside her owner, Steve Drottar - took first place in the stand-up paddleboarding category.
Hundreds of spectators cheered for the dogs as they made a splash
The winners were announced by Rep. Sam Liccardo, former San Jose mayor, to a crowd of hundreds, who cheered for the pups all day from the shore.
The oldest contestant was 11-year-old Charlie, a yellow Lab from the San Diego area, whose surfing journey actually began by accident when owner Jedd Niebor placed the dog onto a surfboard for a quick photo, which turned into an impromptu surf lesson.
Spectators are encouraged to sponsor contestants each year, with a portion of all proceeds going to a number of local charities.

Air Canada suspended flights to and from Tel Aviv (TLV) and Dubai (DXB) until mid-January 2027, the airline announced on Tuesday.
The airline had previously extended its flight pause on these routes until October 24, 2026, citing instability in the region.
Air Canada originally canceled flights to Israel and the United Arab Emirates on March 2, 2026, shortly after the war with Iran began on February 28.
The airline said that it will continue to assess conditions to determine when service will resume.
The Canadian government's official travel guidelines advise citizens to "avoid non-essential travel" to a variety of Middle Eastern nations due to security concerns arising from the conflict with Iran.
Travel advisories to Israel across many nations
These include travel advisories on the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan, and Oman, which are listed under "Orange" or Level 3 advisories that are considered to be "volatile."
Canada also advised its citizens to avoid non-essential travel to Israel, and advises that all travel to Palestinian territories, the border with Lebanon, and the border with Syria be avoided due to "ongoing military operations."
The United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, as well as a number of other countries, also have travel advisories for citizens considering traveling to Israel.

Hezbollah launched a drone at IDF soldiers, the military chose not to tell the public - report

Hezbollah launched an explosive drone near IDF soldiers north of the Beaufort Castle area on Friday, but the IDF did not report the incident, according to Israeli media outlets.
There were no casualties, and the drone caused no damage, media reports said.
Following media inquiries, the military released a statement saying: "The IDF releases information to the public based on factual considerations and situational assessments. The IDF spokesperson will not publish information that, based on a situation assessment, could harm our forces operating in combat zones."
Despite not reporting the incident through its usual public channels, the IDF reportedly confirmed the details to Ynet when asked. The decision not to publicize the drone incident was made by IDF Northern Command chief Maj.-Gen. Rafi Milo, Ynet claimed.
The IDF Spokesperson's Unit regularly publishes updates on activity across Israel's warfronts, including both Israeli military operations and enemy attacks.
Two IDF reservists killed after booby-trapped house explodes in southern Lebanon
These reports came as the IDF launched a probe into the explosion in southern Lebanon which killed two IDF soldiers – Maj. (res.) Harel Birenstock and M.-Sgt. (res.) Tamir Vaknin – and wounded four more.
The explosion occured during a combat mission in southern Lebanon on Wednesday, the military announced on Thursday morning.
Birenstock, 34, from Efrat, was a company commander in Battalion 2855 of the 55th Reserve Paratroopers Brigade, while Vaknin, 33, from Eilat, served as a combat soldier in the same battalion.
Rambam Medical Center said on Thursday morning that the conditions of the four injured soldiers were serious, adding in the afternoon that one of them regained consciousness.
The IDF called the explosion a “blatant violation” of the ceasefire, and struck weapon storage sites and command centers in southern Lebanon, while also issuing evacuation warnings to the town of Mansouri. The Lebanese health ministry said on Wednesday one person was killed and 12 others were wounded in an Israeli strike on the southern town of Tibnin, about 25 kilometers from Mansouri.
