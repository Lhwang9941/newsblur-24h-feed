Belarus ban of Euronews sparks debate and support for press freedom across Europe

A decision by Belarus earlier this week to place Euronews on its list of "extremist" media and ban access to the channel has sparked a wave of support for the media organisation across the continent.
“The Lukashenka regime’s decision to brand Euronews an 'extremist' media outlet is not an act of strength. It is an admission of fear,” said George Papandreou, a former Prime Minister of Greece and current General Rapporteur on Democracy for the Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe.
“Authoritarian regimes do not fear the media-they fear the media they cannot control. They fear independent journalism because it empowers free citizens with facts instead of propaganda.”
Earlier this week, the Belarusian information ministry placed Euronews’ website on its "Republican List of Extremist Materials," according to Belarus-based media. The ruling is subject to immediate execution under Belarusian civil procedure rules.
The decision means access to the Euronews website will now be restricted in Belarus, while readers could face administrative penalties for storing or distributing its content.
“Wherever there is a lack of freedom, free and independent reporting is quickly suspected of extremism. In that respect, the move by the Lukashenka regime comes as no real surprise,” said leader of the German Liberals and former Vice President of the Bundestag, Wolfgang Kubicki.
“For Belarusian civil society, however, this decision is a bitter blow, as it means the loss of an independent source of information.”
Euronews said the designation risks criminalising its audience as well as limiting access to its factual journalism.
"Belarus' decision to designate Euronews as an ‘extremist media outlet’ and ban access to it within the country is yet another blow to press freedom and the free flow of information,” said Cypriot journalist and MEP, Loucas Fourlas.
“As a Member of the European Parliament, a member of the Committee on Civil Liberties and the Committee on Culture and Education, and as a journalist, I stand in solidarity with Euronews and its employees."
Belarus authorities have yet to announce the decision and their arguments that led to Euronews and several other digital media outlets being labelled “extremist media” in Belarus.
Euronews’ television channel broadcast was banned by Belarus authorities in 2021 on a legal procedural basis, before the Kremlin decided to cut Euronews’ television and digital output across the Russian Federation right after Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine.
"The blocking of free media outlets such as Euronews – first in Russia and now in Belarus – is a clear sign of opposition to freedom of expression and diversity,” said Roderich Kiesewetter, a member of the German parliament and the German Council on Foreign Relations.
“Euronews must continue to be a symbol of media freedom and freedom of expression and must not allow itself to be intimidated.”
“Decisions of this kind, which seek to silence and intimidate media organisations, are unacceptable and cannot go unanswered,” said Greek MEP Yannis Maniatis.
“The European Union must continue to stand firmly with journalists, independent media, and the democratic opposition who are striving for a free and democratic Belarus.”
The International and European Federation of journalists (IFJ, EFJ) said in a statement that they "condemned the decision to ban Euronews from broadcasting in Belarus as a new attempt by the Belarusian government to curb press freedom and restrict the right of its citizens to freedom of information."
Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty pointed out that Belarus’ President Aliaksandr Lukashenka “gave interviews to Euronews four times between 2009 and 2014” and that “during an address to the OSCE, he stated that ‘every citizen here watches Euronews.’”
And in a post on X, exiled Belarusian journalist Hanna Liubakova said, “When a dictatorship calls journalism ‘extremism,’ it's admitting the truth is its greatest threat.”
'Shameful' ban
Earlier on Friday, Belarus' opposition leader Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya condemned Minsk's decision, describing it as a “shameful” attempt by Aliaksandr Lukashenka’s government to cut Belarusians off from its factual and independent reporting.
“Lukashenka’s regime has branded Euronews, a leading European news platform, ‘extremist’ for bringing the truth to millions of Belarusians,” she wrote in a post on X.
“This attacks media freedom and turns viewers into targets of repression,” she added, urging the broadcaster to “stay strong” and underlining that: “your work matters.”
Claus Strunz, Euronews’ editorial director, said on Friday the outlet had not been notified of the decision and was awaiting arguments from the authorities.
“A state that brands Euronews as ‘extremist’ and blocks access to independent, impartial journalism reveals its own fear of free reporting,” Strunz said.
He added that the outlet would continue to provide “factual, independent journalism” for its audience in Belarus.
The independent Belarusian Association of Journalists suggested that the suspension of Euronews broadcasts was linked to attempts to restrict access to sources of information.

Texas Republican sparks social media storm with post telling Americans to ‘stop whining’ and ‘get a job’
Outgoing GOP congressman Dan Crenshaw provokes uproar by pushing back against young conservatives concerned about affordability
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
Texas Republican Rep. Dan Crenshaw has found himself at the centre of a social media storm after belittling voters’ concerns about the affordability crisis.
Crenshaw, who was primaried for his seat and lost to GOP state Rep. Steve Toth in March, weighed in on a debate on X about soaring grocery prices after Turning Point USA spokesman Andrew Kolvert said he had recently been told by a college student: “A burrito shouldn’t cost $20.”
“A lot of this is a hangover from Covid and Biden-era inflation, but the lived experience is the same: It just feels like basic things cost too much,” Kolvert observed.
The outgoing congressman and former Navy SEAL said: “Just hearing about this dumb $20 burrito debate so just thought I’d make the losers on social media angry before going to bed.
“Stop whining, get a job, eat Ramen like the rest of us did in college, on a budget with 4 roommates.
“The market doesn’t care what you think something ‘should’ cost. Only communists care (they set prices remember? Then everyone starves, remember?) Are you a communist? Or are you an American adult male capable of handling his own s***?”
The “ragebait” response provoked a storm of anger and derision, notably from Crenshaw’s fellow conservatives, with one characteristic comment pointing out that the price of 10lbs of hamburger meat had tripled between 2020 and 2026.
Several people pointed to the Republican’s own contradictory past output on the same subject, including one tweet from September 2021 in which he had written: “America: our food prices are too high what are you doing about inflation? Biden admin: just be vegan and stop complaining you bunch of rubes.”
Forced into a climbdown, Crenshaw said: “Okay, I am SORRY for triggering the comment section. Of course the world is objectively harder than EVER, and while a $20 burrito is rare, the fact that it exists anywhere is obviously proof that capitalism has FAILED.
“lol no just kidding. Remember when the right had principles and made fun of leftists for elevating victimhood as a virtue? Want to tell people the truth? Or just bribe them for their vote in the next election?”
He was not the only conservative to get caught up in the storm.
Right-leaning columnist Marc Thiessen responded equally unsympathetically to Kolvert, writing: “Cry me a river. The burritos in the college cafeteria are included in your meal plan.”
That drew a response from Vice President JD Vance, who, rather than offering anything more constructive, replied by fat-shaming Thiessen: “I’m surprised to hear Mark [sic] say this. If you’ve ever met him in person, it’s quite obvious the man has never missed a burrito.”
Vance in turn was hit with a series of unflattering photographs of himself as a portly, beardless younger man, as well as an AI image in which he was portrayed devouring a corndog.
Voters have consistently told pollsters they believe the Trump administration – which Vance aspires to inherit – is not doing enough to address the inflated cost of living, which threatens to cause major problems for Republican candidates come November’s midterms.
