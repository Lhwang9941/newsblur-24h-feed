UK PM Burnham vows to end ‘subscription traps’ amid cost-of-living crisis
British consumers are currently spending around US$2.2 billion a year on subscriptions ‘they don’t actually want’, Downing Street said
British Prime Minister Andy Burnham has vowed to crack down on hard-to-cancel subscriptions and fake discounts as part of measures to tackle the cost-of-living crisis, Downing Street said on Sunday.
The announcement came as the new Labour leader was set to return to work after a holiday and begin a tour of the country to talk to people about their financial pressures, his office said.
Burnham said in a statement that “people are sick and tired of rip-off discounts and subscription traps”, where subscriptions are difficult to cancel or automatically renew at a higher cost.
He said his government would be “making it as easy to leave a subscription as it is to join”.
Currently British consumers are “spending an estimated £1.6 billion (US$2.2 billion) a year on [subscriptions] they don’t actually want”, Downing Street said.
New rules will come into force in January next year, it said, forcing businesses to give customers “clearer upfront information, regular reminders and a much easier exit to contracts”.
There will be a new 14-day cooling-off period to let consumers cancel after a trial or a long-term contract renews, the government announced, without describing how this would be implemented.

Interim Carlton coach Josh Fraser has continued the club's mid-season revival with a 10th win from 12 matches, defeating St Kilda on Sunday.
Fans were chanting for Fraser at Docklands Stadium on Sunday, but the 43-year-old has still not formally thrown his hat into the ring \to be full-time coach.
What's next?
The Blues sit eight points clear in 10th with games against the Western Bulldogs and Fremantle to come.
Carlton fans are chanting Josh Fraser's name and a similar endorsement from the club's hierarchy for the Blues' interim coach to take the reins full time could follow as soon as this week.
Fraser has mounted an irrepressible case to take on the vacant head coach role, having delivered 10 wins and just two losses since stepping up when Michael Voss was sacked in mid-May, with the Blues at 1-8.
Fraser, who earned "we want Fraser" chants from Carlton fans after Sunday night's 44-point win, is yet to officially throw his hat in the ring — but that conversation is imminent.

Half-back Adam Cerra joked whether the interim boss should take the reins was the hardest question at the club at the moment.
"Obviously the club's got their processes. We've got full faith in that process and their decision and we'll support whatever they pick. 'Frase' has been great," he said.
"The boys obviously love playing with him. We'll leave that to the club."
Cerra put Carlton's turnaround down to a combination of Fraser's influence and how the playing group lifted him — with experienced players leading from the front and young players expressing themselves.
"Frase has probably allowed a bit of freedom for us to do that, a bit of simplicity in the way we're trying to play," Cerra said.
"It hasn't set too many boundaries on us. It's more trying to explore what our best football looks like and building that week to week.
"These last couple of weeks we've been getting more confidence in our process and our trust and executing."
Fraser was asked whether part of his thinking in holding off on nominating was around managing if he missed out on the job while preparing the team for a potential wildcard match.
"It's a fair question, but no, I haven't thought too deeply about that," the 43-year-old said.
"I've just tried to stay as narrow as I can and it's been challenging with a lot of the questioning and different things, but I still feel such a responsibility to this role as interim coach.
"That's what I needed to and have focused all of my energy on — because I would be doing a disservice, not only to our players, but our staff and everyone, if I started to stray away from that.
"Clearly I've had to think through some things, but that hasn't occupied a lot of my thinking and all the hypotheticals haven't either. Most of my energy has been focused on this playing group and footy club."
