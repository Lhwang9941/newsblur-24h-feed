The New York City Police Department (NYPD) is criticizing Mayor Zohran Mamdani for creating what officials describe as a security nightmare ahead of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's visit to New York in September, according to a report by The New York Post.
Netanyahu is expected to arrive in the city next month for the United Nations General Assembly and is scheduled to address the assembly on September 26.
NYPD sources told the NYP that Netanyahu's visit will require substantial, presidential-level security. They specifically pointed to Mamdani’s recent public statements against the prime minister, which include calls for his arrest and encouragement for the public to protest Netanyahu's presence as sources of their security concerns.
“Basically, he’s turning what would be considered a Category 1 hurricane that we deal with every year into a Category 5 because of his words,” a NYPD source told the NYP.
“We have to protect hotels; we have to protect wherever dignitaries go out to eat; we have to protect everything,” the source added. “Having the mayor say, ‘Go ahead and protest, and he should be arrested’ is unfair to your own officers.”
Netanyahu’s complete schedule for his visit to New York is not yet known, so the final number of police officers required for his security detail is still uncertain.
However, up to 60 or more officers could end up being a part of his security detail, including both direct and peripheral personnel, according to another NYPD source.
Police express concern over Netanyahu's safety
“What’s going to stop one of the protesters from becoming a vigilante and trying to hurt or, God forbid, kill him?” the source questioned.
Officers are also reportedly worried that protesters who believe they are acting on the mayor’s behalf “could cause an officer to get hurt or even killed,” the source stated to the NYP.
Another NYPD source reminded the NYP about the bomb that was discovered outside Gracie Mansion in March. Two men from Pennsylvania, reportedly inspired by ISIS, allegedly attempted to detonate bombs in a crowd during the "Stop the Islamic Takeover of New York City" protest.
The source expressed concern about the possibility of a similar attempted terrorist attack during Netanyahu's visit. “His rhetoric is reckless,” the official stated. “It emboldens radical and unstable agitators who could endanger both New Yorkers and law enforcement officers.”
A New York City government source also shared concerns about the recent increase in antisemitism with the NYP, linking it to Mamdani's stance on Israel.
“It’s very concerning,” said the insider. “I mean, [Mamdani’s] rhetoric has clearly caused this uptick in antisemitism all across our city, and now telling people to go out and protest is going to create a powder keg situation.”
“It’s not the mayor’s job to tell people to protest and to urge them to do so,” the city government source concluded. “It’s likely to be very violent. And I mean, what on Earth is this accomplishing?”
Mamdani's history of anti-Netanyahu rhetoric
Mamdani's anti-Netanyahu rhetoric has been a consistent theme throughout both his campaign and his tenure in political office. During his 2025 New York City mayoral campaign, the Mayor pledged to arrest Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and stated that he would seek to enforce International Criminal Court (ICC) warrants if elected.
He reiterated this pledge in a recent interview with The New York Times in July.
“I believe that Prime Minister Netanyahu belongs in The Hague. Whatever the law allows me to do in New York City, that’s what we will do, but we won’t be writing our own laws to that end,” Mamdani said.
However, later that week, Mamdani was compelled to admit that his administration lacks the authority to execute the ICC's arrest warrant for Netanyahu, as stated in a video released by the mayor's office.
Despite this, he continued to accuse Netanyahu of being responsible for an alleged “genocide against the Palestinian people” and for blocking humanitarian aid to Gaza, adding that Netanyahu “is not welcome in New York City.”

Several haredi (ultra-Orthodox) protesters blocked a road in the area of Meron on Saturday due to buses operating prior to the end of Shabbat, N12 News reported.
Footage published by N12 showed multiple nearby civilians dragging the protesters out of the way and attempting to escort a bus through the demonstration.
Walla noted that the incident marks the sixth consecutive week in which haredi demonstrators blocked traffic to protest public transportation operating on Shabbat.
The Israel Police attempted to disperse the protest, added Walla, with secular counterprotesters arriving on scene as well.
Anti-government protests planned for Saturday night across northern Israel
Further protests in northern Israel are scheduled for Saturday night, Maariv reported, though these are intended as demonstrations against the government.
According to Maariv, the areas expected to face protesters include Guma Junction, Kibbutz Eilon, Afula, Kabri, Nahariya, Tzemach, Rosh Pina, and Karmiel.
Additional anti-government protests are expected throughout the country.

ISTANBUL, August 8. /TASS/. Russia's Yegor Gromadsky has won the gold at the 2026 European Modern Pentathlon Championships in Istanbul with 1,606 points.
Hungary's Botond Tamas finished second with 1,599 points, while Italy's Giorgio Malan took the bronze with 1,592 points.
The tournament runs through August 9. Russian athletes are competing under the national flag and with the national anthem.
