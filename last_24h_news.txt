Princess Eugenie has announced the birth of a baby girl.
Eugenie, who is married to Jack Brooksbank, made the announcement on her Instagram page on Tuesday.
This is the couple’s third child, following sons August and Ernest, and it is understood they are taking time to decide on a name.
In a short post, Eugenie, who is the daughter of Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor, wrote: “Jack and I are so excited to announce Baby Girl Brooksbank!!”
“We are beyond in love with our girlie.”
The birth took place at a hospital in Lisbon, Portugal, on Monday evening, Buckingham Palace said.
Buckingham Palace said: “Her Royal Highness Princess Eugenie and Mr Jack Brooksbank are delighted to announce the safe arrival of their daughter, born on Monday August 3 2026, at 6.20pm, at a hospital in Lisbon, Portugal.
“The baby was born weighing 6lb 9oz.
“Their Majesties The King and Queen and other members of the royal family were delighted to be informed of the news.”

Deadly migrant rush to Spain’s Ceuta was sparked by runaway online rumours
Convinced by social media that Europe was within reach, some 72,000 people surged across the border from Morocco
It started with Instagram posts that suggested Spain’s border with Morocco was opening up. Then came Facebook users tracking coastguard patrols and TikTok videos showing where to swim.
After many shares and comments, thousands of Moroccans became convinced that Europe was suddenly within reach.
Within hours, young people began heading towards the border with the Spanish territory of Ceuta in North Africa. Packed buses rolled into the neighbouring Moroccan city of Fnideq. Regional trains filled up. Taxis did too.
By the time authorities reacted, some 72,000 people had reached Ceuta in just a few days, the largest migration surge between Morocco and Spain in years. More than 80 migrants died on both sides of the border, including some who drowned and others who were killed in a stampede, authorities said.
Nearly all of the surviving migrants soon returned, but the fallout from last week’s events will linger.
In the northern Moroccan city of Larache, about 150km (93 miles) from Ceuta, three friends watched a video posted by an Instagram account called Haraga Ceuta claiming the border would be open.

Trump’s former lawyer advances in attorney general bid after deal with Republican holdouts
Blanche, one of Trump’s most trusted legal aides, confirmed in writing that US$1.8bn settlement for US president’s allies was scrapped
The closely scrutinised Senate Judiciary Committee vote followed two hours of debate about Blanche’s relationship with Trump and the unusual settlement that almost derailed his confirmation, with Republicans voting in favour and Democrats opposing the nomination by a 12-10 vote. Blanche didn’t participate in the meeting as it was not a hearing.
Republican senators John Cornyn of Texas and Thom Tillis of North Carolina had delayed the committee vote for a week. They threatened to oppose Blanche unless he dismantled the proposed “Anti-Weaponisation Fund” and narrowed an agreement limiting Internal Revenue Service audits of Trump and his family.
The fund emerged from the Trump administration’s settlement of a lawsuit the president filed against the IRS over the leaking of his tax records.
It was intended to compensate people who claimed politically motivated government investigations had targeted them. However, critics warned that it could be used to benefit Trump allies, including defendants charged over the January 6, 2021, attack on the US Capitol.
