Spain hit back at Italy as border control rift continues
- Spain has implemented border controls on all travellers from Italy, following Italy's earlier imposition of similar measures.
- Italy initially introduced border checks due to a significant influx of migrants from Morocco into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta, stating it would not yield to 'ultimatums' from Spain.
- Madrid had demanded Rome lift its border checks, calling them 'unjustified' and threatening 'proportionate measures' if Italy failed to comply.
- Italy justified its decision by citing concerns over security, terrorism risks, and preventing irregular migrants from entering European territory via Spain.
- The dispute underscores a growing rift between Italy's conservative Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, who advocates for tighter immigration controls, and Spain's Socialist leader Pedro Sanchez, who generally supports a more liberal approach.

Morocco: Seven taxi drivers jailed in 'harsh first ruling' after Ceuta migrant surge
Seven Moroccan taxi drivers were sentenced to up to six months in prison and fined $1,070 each for aiding irregular migration by driving people to the Spanish border in Ceuta. It marked the first ruling in Morocco tied to the surge of more than 70,000 people crossing into the tiny Spanish North African territory over the past week.
Judges in the northern Moroccan city of Tetouan found the taxi drivers guilty of "facilitating the illegal exit of Moroccans and foreigners through unauthorised border points" and "transporting passengers without a license," after they drove clients to the Moroccan border town of Fnideq, according to excerpts of the ruling on the Justice Ministry website.
The drivers were sentenced on Monday after appearing before prosecutors without defence representation due to a nationwide lawyers’ strike. They retain the right to appeal. One vehicle was confiscated by authorities.
Dozens in pre-trial detention 
The Moroccan Association of Human Rights said on Friday that 111 people are in pretrial detention following the recent Ceuta events. Charges include mass rebellion, assaulting public officials and obstruction of traffic, it said.
Morocco’s Justice Ministry and the public prosecutor’s office did not immediately respond to requests for comment.
Labour unions, including the national taxi union, condemned the sentences as "shocking and harsh," noting in a statement that "no official decision was issued imposing restrictions on passenger transport".
"Taxi drivers cannot be held responsible for what happened," Ali Younes, secretary general of the Tetouan branch of the national taxi union told The Associated Press. "They were punished simply for providing a public service."
Fueled by false social media rumours that the border was open and economic hardship, tens of thousands of Moroccans crossed or swam into Ceuta, a Spanish city that sits on the North African coast, triggering a crisis resonating across Europe.
Nearly all of the migrants who survived were expelled by Spanish authorities or returned voluntarily, while an estimated 1,000 minors remain, their future uncertain.
Officials in Spain take apart a major trafficking network 
Meanwhile, European Union police agency Europol on Friday announced that an international police operation led by Spanish authorities dismantled a major criminal network involved in trafficking migrants, drugs and weapons across the Western Mediterranean. The operation resulted in 78 arrests in Spain and Algeria in June, weeks before the migrant surge into Ceuta.

Toddler at center of Texas brain-death dispute is taken off life support and dies
The family has not publicly stated the reasons behind the decision to remove her from life support
- Bookmark
A two-year-old girl, whose parents successfully sued a Texas hospital to prevent brain-death testing after she drowned in a hotel pool, has died after being removed from life support.
Annelise Camp’s family confirmed her passing this week, weeks after a judge granted a temporary injunction in their favor.
Her parents, Johnston and Joy Camp, released a statement saying Annelise "is now with Jesus." They added, "While our hearts are broken, we trust in His perfect love, His faithfulness, and His promises."
Annelise was found unconscious in a hotel pool on Memorial Day and subsequently admitted to Texas Children's Hospital in Houston.
Days later, her parents petitioned a judge to block doctors from conducting brain-death tests, asserting she still showed signs of life and deserved more time to recover and explore treatment options.
Court documents, as reported by The Texas Tribune, indicated Annelise’s heart was beating, though she relied on a ventilator.
The Camps also sought to transfer her to another facility for alternative treatments.
Texas Children’s, however, maintained its desire to perform testing to guide her medical care, stating it had no immediate plans to cease treatment.
Hospital officials noted they had contacted 36 medical facilities regarding a transfer, with 35 declining and the remaining one requiring brain-death testing before consideration.
The family eventually secured a transfer for Annelise to a hospital in Louisiana.
State legislator Rep. Steve Toth informed The New York Times that Annelise was later brought back to Texas about a week ago, receiving care at Christus Mother Frances Hospital in Tyler.
Annelise’s grandfather, Steve Camp, posted online that her breathing tubes were removed on Monday, and "When this procedure was done, she passed very quickly."
The family has not publicly stated the reasons behind the decision to remove her from life support.
The family’s legal battle garnered support from organizations like Texas Right to Life, which stated online, "This victory gave the Camp family two extra months with Annelise and provided her a chance to recover from her injury."
Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton also weighed in, posting on X in June, "Annelise Camp is fighting for her life and deserves a medical team that will fight just as hard."
Texas law defines brain death as the irreversible cessation of brain function, allowing hospitals to withdraw life-sustaining measures once a patient is declared brain-dead.
Johnston Camp told KRIV-TV in June, "The big common denominator is time. And so when you have time, I know scientifically you can see a certain reading, but she is showing progressive results from her body or complexions, her vitals."
In July, the Camps had shared on a GoFundMe page that Annelise continued to show "encouraging signs" at the Louisiana hospital, adding, "We are pursuing every available option, including specialized and exploratory treatments that show promise."
A church service for Annelise is scheduled for next Wednesday in Houston.
