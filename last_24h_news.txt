Taylor Swift song ‘August’ removed from Trump TikTok
The president has made public digs at the ‘Love Story’ singer for years
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
A TikTok video posted by Donald Trump’s campaign that included Taylor Swift’s song “August” has had the sound removed since it was posted four days ago.
The TikTok account @TeamTrump shared a video soundtracked to the song, depicting Donald and Melania Trump standing on a balcony watching fireworks with the text: “Mood because it’s August and Donald Trump is your president.”
The caption reads: “I’m sure @Taylor Swift is going to be super excited we used her song! #taylorswift #maga #august.”
Fans noticed Friday that Swift’s track, from her 2020 album Folklore, has since been removed from the post by the copyright owner.
In the U.S. version of the TikTok app, a message on the video reads: “The copyright owner hasn’t made this sound available in your country.”
After the song was stripped from the post, one of Swift’s fans showed support by commenting: “TAYLOR REMOVING THE AUDIO QUEEEEEEN.”
“Is the song with us right now?” said another.
The Independent has contacted representatives for Swift and Trump for comment.
The Team Trump TikTok account, which has 14 million followers, was created ahead of the 2024 U.S. presidential election.
A video posted by the account in November that included Swift’s song “Father Figure” has also had the audio removed.
The Team Trump account has appeared to troll Swift further, posting a screenshot of the tracklist for Swift’s album Red, but with the text and images altered in favor of Trump.
The album’s title was changed to Red (Donald Trump’s Version) while song titles included “Red (Taylor Swift Wrote A Whole Album About Republicans)” and “We Are Never Ever Getting Back Together (The Biden Harris Administration).”
It is not the first time the Trump administration has made a public dig at the world-renowned singer. On her July 4th wedding day, the White House issued a social media post parodying the billboards that were displayed in Manhattan’s Midtown announcing that the singer and Travis Kelce had tied the knot at Madison Square Garden.
“IT’S HAPPENED!!!” the White House’s official X account said in a post, along with an AI-generated picture of pink billboards saying, “Trump is your president.”
Trump and Swift have had bad blood for years. The feud escalated in 2024 after Swift endorsed Trump’s opponent Kamala Harris for president by saying, “I think she is a steady-handed, gifted leader and I believe we can accomplish so much more in this country if we are led by calm and not chaos.”
The endorsement enraged Trump and caused him to write on social media: “I HATE TAYLOR SWIFT!”
He went on to warn that Swift would “pay a price for it in the marketplace.”
In 2025, he mocked her again on his platform by writing, “Has anyone noticed that, since I said ‘I HATE TAYLOR SWIFT,’ she’s no longer ‘HOT?’”
However, he seemed to cool off later in the year. When news of Swift and Kelce’s engagement was announced in August, Trump congratulated the couple while speaking to reporters at a cabinet meeting.
“Well, I wish them a lot of luck,” Trump said. “I think it’s — I think he’s a great player, I think he’s a great guy and I think that she’s a terrific person. So I wish them a lot of luck.”

Royal Navy warns of 25% surge in Russian naval activity near UK
The Royal Navy spent 21 days in July monitoring Moscow’s vessels
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
The Royal Navy has warned of a significant increase in Russian naval activity near UK waters, having spent 21 days in July monitoring Moscow's vessels.
Naval operations to track Russian ships surged by 25 per cent in July compared to last year, with warships repeatedly traversing the Dover Strait and North Sea.
Offshore patrol vessels HMS Tyne, HMS Mersey and HMS Severn were deployed throughout the month to monitor Russian frigates Neustrashimy and Admiral Grigorovich, which are tasked with escorting so-called “shadow fleet” tankers.
Russia uses its ageing shadow fleet to evade international sanctions on its oil exports and help fund its war in Ukraine.
HMS Tyne was also tasked with monitoring the Neustrashimy as it carried out a live firing exercise outside British and French waters on July 20.
Another ship, the frigate HMS Somerset, has been involved in monitoring Russian activity in the North Atlantic as part of a four-month Nato deployment searching for submarines, alongside helicopters from the Navy’s Fleet Air Arm.
Somerset’s commanding officer, Commander Matt Millyard, said: “This deployment demonstrates exactly what the Royal Navy exists to do, protect the UK and our allies from wherever our interests are threatened.
“I am immensely proud of every single member of the ship’s company for what we have achieved over the past four months.
It is fantastic to bring the ship home after a highly successful deployment and reunite with our families.”
The increase in Russian naval activity follows the seizure of the tanker Smyrtos by Royal Marines in June, the first time British troops have directly boarded a shadow fleet vessel.
The Ministry of Defence (MoD) said the diversion of Russian ships to escort shadow fleet vessels meant Russia was “using up valuable operational capacity to constantly guard cargo”.
Last month, the MoD said in its annual report that Royal Navy vessels had been “activated” 116 times in the year to March 2026, shadowing 61 Russian warships and 28 merchant vessels around the UK’s waters.
This reflected a 30 per cent increase in Russian naval activity over the previous two years, and included operations to deter the spy ship Yantar that was spotted near undersea cables off Scotland in November 2025.

US has below 1,700 Patriot missiles left amid Iran operation — newspaper
Unknown
Unknown Publisher
2026-08-08
21:05:54 UTC
06:05:54 KST

NEW YORK, August 8. /TASS/. The US military operation against Iran has significantly depleted American arsenals, leaving the Pentagon with fewer than 1,700 Patriot interceptor missiles, The New York Times reported, citing sources.
According to the report, the Donald Trump administration "has worked with military contractors to try to accelerate and expand production but even accounting for those efforts, it could take more than two years for the United States to replenish the more than 1,500 Patriot air-defense interceptor missiles that have been used in the Iran war." Two people briefed on the matter told the newspaper that the current US stockpile stood at fewer than 1,700 missiles.
Dwindling inventories are forcing the Pentagon to redeploy ships, aircraft and air defense units from Asia and Europe to the Middle East, a move that the newspaper said was having "cascading consequences" for equipment maintenance and troop morale. According to the report, some US officials fear South Korea and Japan will doubt the ability of the United States to protect them.
The Washington Post reported on August 5, citing sources, that US President Donald Trump had demanded answers from Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth on July 31 over why he had allegedly been misled about severe ammunition shortages during the conflict with Iran. The newspaper said the exchange highlighted Trump’s growing frustration with Hegseth. Trump later denied the allegations.
