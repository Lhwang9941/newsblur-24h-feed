Over 200 firefighters battle blaze in southern France
Officials said the fire, which forced evacuations, might have been sparked by a car catching fire on a local road
A fast-moving wildfire was raging in southern France on Sunday, forcing evacuations as more than 200 firefighters raced to contain the blaze.
The fire broke out at around 12.45pm in the Lozere region, about 140km (85 miles) northwest of Montpellier.
Officials said it might have been sparked by a car catching fire on a local road.
By late afternoon, the fire had burned some 90 hectares (220 acres) and continued to spread.
Around 220 firefighters from Lozere and neighbouring departments were deployed, backed by four Canadair water bombers and a Dash aircraft, with more reinforcements on the way.
“The situation is not under control,” local fire services said.

Israel’s 'New East' Golan army project nearly 80% complete
Israel’s army says it has completed nearly 80% of excavation work on a major military fortification project along the occupied Syrian Golan Heights, according to an Israeli media report, as Israel continues military incursions and attacks inside Syrian territory.
The project, known in Hebrew as Mizrah Hadash or "New East", consists of deep anti-vehicle trenches and earth berms running along the frontier with Syria. Ynet reported on Friday that the works began around two years ago and that the excavations are close to completion, citing the Israeli military during a field visit to the area.
According to the report, the trenches are around four metres deep and four metres wide, designed to obstruct armed groups from entering Israeli-controlled territory by car or motorcycle. The Israeli army has said the project is intended to delay any attempted cross-border incursion and give forces time to respond.
Prominent Kurdish leader Selahattin Demirtas, jailed in Turkey since 2016, on Sunday backed a bill that would grant limited and conditional amnesty to PKK Kurdish militants ahead of a parliamentary vote on Monday.
"We hope that you will take another crucial step forward in this historic peace effort," the former co-chair of Turkey's main pro-Kurdish party said in a statement.
He said the law would "radically transform the fate of our country and our region" after more than four decades of armed conflict that has claimed at least 50,000 lives.
The bill, which cleared parliament's committee stage on Friday, would allow PKK fighters to return to civilian life under certain conditions, following the group's announcement last year that it was disbanding at the request of its jailed founder and longtime leader Abdullah Ocalan.
The bill, backed by President Recep Tayyip Erdogan's ruling Justice and Development Party (AKP) and the pro-Kurdish DEM opposition party, would suspend prosecutions and sentences for perpetrators of certain offences for a period of between five and ten years.
If those covered by the measure do not reoffend during that period, investigations would be dropped and sentences considered served.
However, some serious crimes such as intentional homicide would be excluded from the scheme.
The text does not say what will happen to Ocalan, 77, who has been in jail since 1999 but has backed a peace process launched in late 2024.
