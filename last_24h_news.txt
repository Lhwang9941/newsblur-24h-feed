Banned driver killed pensioner after crashing stolen Maserati into garden
- Fraser Garden, 55, has admitted causing the death of 74-year-old John Bell by dangerous driving after stealing a high-powered Maserati from a car showroom in Broughty Ferry on April 13.
- Garden drove the stolen vehicle at speeds exceeding 80mph through a residential area, nearly three times the 30mph limit, also jumping a red light and driving on the wrong side of the road.
- He lost control of the Maserati, clipping a kerb and striking a traffic island before ploughing into a garden where Mr Bell was working, pinning him against a house wall and causing "non-survivable" injuries.
- The court heard Garden had 90 previous convictions, including numerous motoring offences, and was disqualified from driving on the day of the incident, with the car still travelling at 34mph upon impact.
- Garden pleaded guilty to five charges, including theft of a motor vehicle and causing death by dangerous driving, and has been told by Judge Lord Braid that a "lengthy custodial sentence is inevitable" ahead of his sentencing on August 21.

The National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG) welcomed progress on the roadmap for the second phase of the Gaza ceasefire on Friday, saying it is ready to assume responsibility for administering the enclave.
In a statement on X, the committee called the development "an important milestone" and said it had completed preparations to restore public services, facilitate humanitarian aid, strengthen the rule of law and lead Gaza's recovery and reconstruction.
The NCAG said its mission, in line with US President Donald Trump's Gaza plan and UN Security Council Resolution 2803, is to establish governance under "one authority, one law, and one security apparatus."
The committee said its immediate priorities include restoring public institutions, improving humanitarian conditions, strengthening public security and creating an environment conducive to economic recovery.
The NCAG said it would work with the Board of Peace, the Office of the High Representative, the International Stabilisation Force and regional and international partners to carry out its responsibilities.
"Our objective extends beyond reconstruction to building a Gaza that is safe, stable, and prosperous," the committee said, adding that the territory should be governed by legitimate Palestinian institutions committed to the Palestinian people's right to self-determination and statehood.
Earlier on Friday, Trump announced that an agreement had been reached for the complete disarmament of Palestinian resistance group Hamas, with Israeli forces set to withdraw in phases.
He credited Egypt, Qatar and Türkiye with helping broker what he described as a "historic breakthrough," saying an International Stabilisation Force and a new Palestinian police force would gradually assume responsibility for security in Gaza.
