TAIPEI (Taiwan News) — Taiwan's average household disposable income reached a record NT$1.21 million (US$38,500) in 2025, up 3.9% from a year earlier, according to a report issued Friday by the Directorate-General of Budget, Accounting and Statistics.
Disposable income refers to income remaining after non-consumption expenditures such as taxes and social insurance premiums have been deducted, leaving money available for consumption or savings, per Storm Media.
Hsinchu retained its position as the municipality with the highest average household disposable income at NT$1.604 million, followed by Hsinchu County at NT$1.569 million. Taipei ranked third at NT$1.554 million per household.
Hsinchu's high ranking was attributed to its concentration of high-tech companies in the Hsinchu Science Park and the growth of the AI industry. Taipei, meanwhile, benefits from its position as Taiwan's political and financial center, per CNA.
Among Taiwan's six major municipalities, Taipei, New Taipei, Taoyuan, and Taichung all recorded average household disposable income above the national average of NT$1.21 million. Tainan and Kaohsiung were below the national average, with household disposable income of NT$1.044 million and NT$1.206 million, respectively.
DGBAS added that average household consumption expenditure reached NT$920,000 in 2025, an increase of 3.6% from 2024. Average household savings rose 4.9% to NT$290,000 during the same period, per the report.

US Representative McCaul warns warns of China's belligerence toward Taiwan

TAIPEI (Taiwan News) — US Rep. Michael McCaul, chair emeritus of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, warned that China's increasingly belligerent threats toward Taiwan pose a direct challenge to the global balance of power during his third visit to Taiwan, per VOA.
McCaul, a Republican from Texas, visited Taiwan from Aug. 2-7, meeting with President Lai Ching-te (賴清德), Vice President Hsiao Bi-khim (蕭美琴), and Foreign Minister Lin Chia-lung (林佳龍). He also delivered a speech at the annual Ketagalan Forum, where he reiterated US support under the Taiwan Relations Act.
"At a time when the stakes couldn't be higher, I was proud to visit Taiwan for the third time to reaffirm the United States' unwavering support," McCaul said in a statement. "The increasing belligerence of the Chinese Communist Party poses a direct threat — not only to Taiwan, but to the entire global balance of power," per a press release.
McCaul said he has consistently supported Taiwan, including efforts to advance the 2024 Supplemental Appropriations Act and strengthen US-Taiwan economic relations. He also highlighted his support for closer security cooperation among the US, Australia, and the United Kingdom. In recognition of his support for Taiwan, Lai awarded McCaul the Grand Cordon of the Order of the Brilliant Star.
McCaul previously announced that he would not seek reelection after his current term in Congress expires.
During his visit, McCaul also met with opposition leaders, including Legislative Yuan Speaker Han Kuo-yu (韓國瑜), and legislators from opposition parties. Discussions covered Taiwan's recently passed defense budget, the importance of drones, and US-Taiwan cooperation on drone production.
Before leaving Taiwan, McCaul also met with officials from the National Security Council and Ministry of National Defense to exchange views on strengthening Taiwan's defenses against China's coercive actions in the region. He also visited US drone company Shield AI and the Industrial Technology Research Institute, where he learned about Taiwan's drone industry and ITRI's certification work.
ITRI is the first institution outside the US authorized to certify that drones do not contain components from China, per VOA.
McCaul, a member of the House of Representatives Taiwan Caucus, first visited Taiwan in April 2023 as chair of the House Foreign Affairs Committee. He returned in May 2024 to attend Lai's inauguration.
China conducted military exercises involving aircraft and naval forces around Taiwan during both of those visits and imposed sanctions on McCaul. During his latest visit, McCaul said China's coercive actions had only strengthened his commitment to supporting a free and peaceful Taiwan.

TAIPEI (Taiwan News) — The Ministry of National Defense tracked 11 Chinese military aircraft, eight naval vessels, and two official ships between 6 a.m. on Saturday and the same time Sunday.
In response, Taiwan deployed aircraft, naval ships, and coastal-based missile systems. Seven of the People’s Liberation Army aircraft crossed the Taiwan Strait median line in the country’s northern and central air defense identification zones, or ADIZ, according to the ministry.
So far this month, Taiwan has tracked Chinese military aircraft 126 times and ships 189 times. Since September 2020, China has increased its use of gray zone tactics by incrementally increasing the number of military aircraft and naval vessels operating around Taiwan.
Gray zone tactics are defined as “an effort or series of efforts beyond steady-state deterrence and assurance that attempts to achieve one’s security objectives without resort to direct and sizable use of force.”

Kim made the remarks in a reply message on Saturday, thanking his Russian counterpart for sending a message of greeting on the occasion of the 81st anniversary of Liberation Day, according to the report.

Mahuchikh wins gold in high jump at 2026 European Athletics Championships
According to Ukrinform, Poland's Maria Zodzik won silver with a clearance of 1.95 meters, while Montenegro's Marija Vukovic took bronze at 1.95 meters, finishing lower based on the number of attempts.
Ukraine's Iryna Herashchenko finished fifth with a result of 1.92 meters.
This is Ukraine's fourth medal at the 2026 European Athletics Championships. Earlier, Mykhailo Kokhan won bronze in the hammer throw, Oleh Doroshchuk took silver in the high jump, and Liudmyla Olianovska won bronze in the half marathon race walk with a national record.
At the previous European Championships in Rome in 2024, Ukrainian athletes won six medals: one gold (Yaroslava Mahuchikh), one silver (Vladyslav Lavskyy), and four bronze medals (Oleh Doroshchuk, Mykhailo Kokhan, Iryna Herashchenko, and Liudmyla Olianovska).
The next European Athletics Championships will be held from June 3 to 8, 2028, at the Silesian Stadium in Chorzow, Poland.
