Iran releases disturbing ‘kill Melania’ video threatening the First Lady and Barron Trump
The Secret Service says it is aware of the clip and is investigating after it was posted by a news agency affiliated with Iran’s Revolutionary Guard
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
The Secret Service is investigating a disturbing video posted in Iran that threatens both Melania and Barron Trump.
The clip, titled ‘Where to kill Melania’ shows a mock up of the First Lady’s motorcade moving through the streets of New York City and calls on “freedom fighters” to take action against her. It concludes with an ominous threat against Trump’s 20-year-old son: “Barron Trump, wait for us!” 
The clip was shared on Monday by the Tasnim News Agency, which has been associated with Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. 
Nate Herring, a spokesperson for the Secret Service, confirmed to Newsweek that the agency is aware of the video. He added that the agency investigates “anything that can be perceived as a threat toward our protectees.”
“Out of concern for operational security, we do not discuss matters of protective intelligence,” he added. The Independent has contacted the White House for further comment.
The video is not the first threat against the Trump family amid the ongoing conflict between the U.S. and Iran. In July, a billboard depicting portraits of Trump, Melania and several of the president’s children above coffins draped in American flags was spotted in Tehran’s Palestine Square.
Another billboard, depicting Trump’s body in an open coffin, was spotted in central Tehran earlier this month. 
Banners reading “We will kill Trump” were brandished by crowds in Mashhad, ahead of the burial of Iran’s former Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, who died in a U.S.-Israeli airstrike on the first day of the war.
Trump has repeatedly claimed that he is a target for Iran, telling reporters at a NATO summit in Turkey that he was Iran’s “No. 1 target.”
“I'm the No. 1, because they're scum,” he said earlier this month. “That's the way they act, and that's the way they've done it for 47 years, but I'm doing what's right for the country.”
Trump later quipped that he liked being “No.1 on TikTok better,” before reiterating that he was Iran’s top target.
Earlier in July, he wrote on Truth Social that orders had been given to the U.S. Military to “completely decimate and destroy all areas of Iran” should the regime attempt to assassinate him. 
“1000 Missiles are Locked and Loaded and aimed at the Islamic Republic of Iran, with thousands of more to immediately follow, should the Iranian Government act on its threat, pronounced in many corners of the Globe, to assassinate, or attempt to assassinate, the sitting President of the United States of America, in this case, ME,” he wrote.
This week Trump said “friendly negotiations” were taking place between the two countries after he paused two weeks of U.S. strikes on Iran, in a bid to resume the fragile peace process.

China woos Slovakia with AI, robotics pitch as EU trade friction mounts
The country has maintained relatively warm ties with Beijing even as the bloc has toughened its stance on trade
Chinese President Xi Jinping said that China and Slovakia should explore cooperation in clean energy, robotics and artificial intelligence, as the European nation navigates growing trade tensions between Beijing and the EU.
Xi told his counterpart Peter Pellegrini that mutual trust has continued to deepen between the nations. The Slovak president is on a three-day visit to China, the first by the country’s head of state in 16 years.
Slovakia has relatively warm relations with China, as one of the few EU and Nato members to have established a strategic partnership with the country in recent years. Ties were raised to that level during Prime Minister Robert Fico’s visit to Beijing in November 2024.
Meanwhile, cracks in the transatlantic relationship under the second Trump administration – particularly the president’s “America first” trade policies – have pushed some European nations to recalibrate their relations with China.

Russian attacks kill 9, injure almost 70 across Ukraine as strikes on gas stations continue
Russian attacks killed at least nine people and injured at least 69 across Ukraine over the past day as it continues a campaign of attacks on gas stations, local authorities said on July 28.
Chernihiv Oblast Governor Viacheslav Chaus said overnight Russian forces damaged a gas station in Kholmy in a Gerbera drone strike. A 21-year-old local resident was injured in the attack.
In an overnight assault, Russian forces launched 131 drones across Ukraine, the Air Force said. It added that Ukrainian air defenses downed 107 drones over the north, south, center, and east of the country.
In eastern Donetsk Oblast, four people were killed and 19 others injured by Russian attacks over the past day, regional governor Vadym Filashkin said. In the city of Kramatorsk, seven people were injured in Russian air attacks, he added. Forty-three residential houses were damaged over the past day.
In southern Kherson Oblast, three people were killed, and 10 others were injured by Russian attacks over the past day, regional governor Oleksandr Prokudin said.
In northeastern Kharkiv Oblast, one person was killed, and 12 others were injured over the past day, regional governor Oleh Syniehubov said.
In the regional capital of Kharkiv, Saltivskyi and Kyivskyi districts came under attack, leaving two people with acute stress reactions and injuring a 49-year-old man and a 46-year-old woman, Syniehubov said.
Russian attacks on Dnipropetrovsk Oblast killed one person and injured seven people over the past day, Governor Oleksandr Hanzha reported according to updates published on July 27 and July 28.
In southeastern Zaporizhzhia Oblast, 14 people were injured by Russian attacks over the past day, regional governor Ivan Fedorov said. Russia launched 16 guided aerial bombs (KAB) attacks and used 824 drones to attack the region, while also launching 263 artillery strikes, according to the governor.
In northeastern Sumy Oblast, six people, including three children, were injured in a Russian KAB attack overnight, Governor Oleh Hryhorov said.
The U.N. Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine (HRMMU) said in a report released July 14 that June was the deadliest month for civilians in Ukraine since April 2022, with at least 293 killed and 1,990 injured.
Long-range missiles and drones remained the leading cause of civilian casualties in June, accounting for 45% of all casualties, with 126 people killed and 907 injured. Most of those attacks struck urban centers far from the front line, including Kyiv and Dnipro, according to the report.
