Three Palestinians were slightly injured during an Israeli settler extremist attack on the West Bank village of Surif overnight on Saturday, according to an Army Radio report.
The extremists allegedly set fire to buildings and cars and attacked Palestinians with clubs and tear gas, in addition to spray-painting threatening graffiti on buildings.
According to Army Radio, IDF troops were dispatched to the area shortly after the attack to search for suspects and collect evidence. The troops located the burned vehicles and graffiti, but no arrests in connection with the attack have been made.
Israel Police also opened an investigation into the incident, Army Radio stated, and officers from the Judea and Samaria District have begun collecting evidence and testimonies.
Investigations into settler extremism result in arrests
On Wednesday, security officials arrested an Israeli resident of the West Bank for allegedly participating in a similar violent extremist attack on Palestinians in Huwara in June.
In the June Huwara attack, several Palestinians were hospitalized for treatment of injuries inflicted by the extremist settlers, many of whom were reportedly wearing masks to hide their identities.
On Monday, the IDF deployed two extra battalions to the West Bank in an effort to curb recent escalation in extremist settler violence and Palestinian terrorist attacks.
Sarah Ben-Nun and Maya Zanger-Nadis contributed to this report.

Trump walks back agreement to give Ukraine license to produce Patriot missiles

US President Donald Trump walked back his decision to let Ukraine produce its own Patriot interceptors in comments to the Financial Times on Thursday.
Despite previously publicly announcing that he would grant Kyiv the license to the surface-to-air interceptor missiles, Trump told the FT that he was “not sure” about the license and that “we’re looking at it.”
“It’s a very extraordinary weapon, and . . . we have to be a little bit careful of who we license to. We don’t really license equipment,” he told the FT.
Last week, Trump and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky had a "positive and productive,” meeting ahead of former US senator Lindsey Graham’s funeral.
Before his meeting with Trump, Zelensky said that his “number-one priority is anti-ballistic defense and strategic cooperation with America.”
Zelensky said Trump "accepted that he will give us licenses"
After the meeting, Zelensky said that Trump had “accepted that he will give us licenses."
That same day, Zelensky met with representatives from Lockheed Martin, which produces the Patriot interceptors, as well as several other ballistic missile systems.
Zelensky wrote on X/Twitter that he discussed “our joint capabilities related to Patriots and other systems.”
“Our teams are already working on specific solutions to move to co-production as quickly as possible and increase our capabilities to protect lives,” he added.
Ukraine has notably become a world leader in drone production and deterrence, but has yet to figure out a way to protect itself from ballistic missiles.
On Friday, Ukraine’s envoy to Washington, Olha Stefanishyna, told reporters that Ukraine was in discussions with the Pentagon about a license to produce Patriot PAC-3 missiles itself, but that process could take 12 months to five years.
Officials from Patriot maker Raytheon Co had already been to Ukraine, and Lockheed Martin Corp, which builds the PAC-3 interceptors for the air defense system, was working to send a team of experts to the country as well, Stefanishyna told an event hosted by the Christian Science Monitor.
"He has an intention to destroy Ukraine, and that's why, you know, we're looking for different capabilities,” she said.
“We have been successful in everything, but so far, for this winter, we need the missiles."
"Make them yourself": Trump told Zelensky he could have license to produce Patriot
In July, Trump told Zelensky that he would give him the license to make the missiles.
"We're going to give a license to you to make Patriots. That's pretty cool. This way, you can't complain that we're not giving 'em enough," Trump said at a meeting with Zelensky at the NATO summit in Ankara.
"It's a defensive weapon, which I like better than an offensive weapon," Trump said.
Trump then told the FT on Thursday that he was more interested in ending the war in Ukraine than in giving Ukraine the defensive weapon.
“Very simply put, we want the war in Ukraine with Russia ended,” he said. “I’m not looking for missiles. We’re looking for peace.”
Reuters contributed to this report.

Almost all of the migrants who entered Spain’s enclave of Ceuta had left by Saturday, according to officials. An estimated 60,000 migrants entered Ceuta from neighbouring Morocco over just a few days, with at least 67 people losing their lives in the attempt. Spain has announced plans to install a 500-metre-long containment barrier along the sea border. Meanwhile, a majority of EU countries have called for emergency talks in an open letter to the bloc’s interior ministers.
