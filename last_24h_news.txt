Biden administration officials predicted that Israel would expand the war in Gaza to be able to strike Iran and Lebanon, sources told The New Yorker.
During the Biden presidency, as part of ongoing clashes with Iran, Israel launched strikes on significant parts of Iranian infrastructure and Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps-backed terror groups in the region, such as Hezbollah.
In a detailed long-form report for the magazine, several former administration officials said that former president Joe Biden reportedly believed that any pressure that the US put on Israel during the war in Gaza would lead to bigger attacks from Iran. This would mean that the US would have to step in to defend Israel, even if it caused a larger regional war.
Biden’s former White House coordinator for the Middle East, Brett McGurk, argued that immovable support for Israel would help contain Iran.
Even though the administration told Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on multiple occasions to refrain from provoking Iran or its terror proxies in the region, he continued to blindside the US administration by launching strikes with no real notification ahead of time.
B<strong>iden officials feared Israel would expand war in Gaza</strong>
For instance, sources told the New Yorker that the Israeli government did not warn the Biden administration before it carried out several strikes on Hezbollah and Iranian officials, including the September 2024 beeper attack.
One former White House official added that Israel assassinating former Hezbollah leader Hassan Nasrallah nearly two days after the US called for Israel and Hezbollah to observe a temporary ceasefire was a “humiliation” for Biden.
However, the former administration did not publicly criticize Israel for the moves, although many times it led to clashes where the US felt compelled to defend Israel.
Most notably in April and October of 2024, when the US helped defend Israel against Iranian strikes during which very few people were injured.
Netanyahu unsuccessfully pushed unwilling Biden officials to attack Iran
However, officials told the New Yorker that Netanyahu wanted to attack more of Iranian infrastructure in additional strikes, and only backed down “reluctantly.”
Former Biden administration officials told the New Yorker that looking back, they regret that the protection the US provided unintentionally led Netanyahu to push boundaries with Israel’s closest ally.
“We could advise them very firmly, and Biden did that with Bibi repeatedly: ‘Goddammit, take the win and don’t drag us into a further war.’ But the Israelis knew we were going to defend them,” Philip Gordon, former national-security adviser to then-vice-president Kamala Harris, told the New Yorker.
Former defense secretary Lloyd Austin reportedly went so far as to compare Netanyahu to a gambler “playing with house money.”
But then, US President Donald Trump was elected to office. One former senior Pentagon official said Biden's team warned the incoming Trump administration that "the Israelis were not going to let this opportunity go."
Within the first half of Trump’s term, the US has engaged twice in conflict with Iran.
In the latest round of fighting, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said that the US struck because American intelligence believed that Israeli strikes on Iran were imminent, which would have launched a subsequent attack on US forces in the Middle East.
“We knew that there was going to be an Israeli action, we knew that they would precipitate an attack against American forces, and we knew that if we didn’t preemptively go after them before they launched those attacks, we would suffer higher casualties,” Rubio told reporters in March.
Though Trump said he “might have forced Israel’s hand” in the strikes earlier this year, the report notes that Rubio’s recount of the reasons to strike Iran was familiar to Biden officials.
The former administration, and potentially the current one, felt as if it could not stop Israel from attacking targets in the region, but felt the need to “shoulder the burden” no matter the outcome.
Gordon told the New Yorker that Washington was in a “moral hazard” due to its staunch support of Israel, which emboldened Netanyahu to expand the war in Gaza to Iran.
Some former senior Biden administration officials told the New Yorker that, at least in some aspects, the Iran war was an extension of the Israel-Hamas War.
Notably, some senior administration officials, McGurk included, argued that public condemnation of Israel, like that which occurred after the World Central Kitchen incident, led to Hamas believing that regional war could be possible.
Additionally, the New Yorker report noted that US intelligence agencies believed that Iran would leave actual combat for its proxies, Hamas, Hezbollah, and the Houthis.
It also noted that the agencies believed that the then-supreme leader of Iran, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, was risk-averse due to his age.
However, some intelligence reports generated after the October 7 massacre suggested that a small number of Iranians believed that this was the moment to wipe Israel off the map.
Now, as Israel stays on alert for a new round of potential fighting with the Islamic Regime, Biden officials told the New Yorker that they blame Trump for allowing the war in Gaza to last until last October, expand to Iran, and cause regional instability.

Associate of Manchester synagogue attacker jailed over terror reconnaissance

The man who conducted hostile reconnaissance at a military base alongside the Manchester synagogue attacker has been jailed.
Mohammad Asim Bashir, 31, was sentenced to life imprisonment with a minimum term of 16 years and 135 days on Thursday. Bashir will also be subject to mandatory terrorism notification requirements for the rest of his life.
Following the terrorist attack that took place at the Heaton Park Hebrew Congregation Synagogue on Yom Kippur, October 2025, Counter Terrorism Policing North West (CTPNW) launched a large-scale investigation to establish the full circumstances behind the attack.
Phones belonging to the attacker, Jihad al-Shamie, were reviewed by CTPNW and identified Bashir as a close associate who also held extremist views.
During WhatsApp conversations between the pair, they discussed preparing for “war” and frequently made antisemitic remarks.
Bashir expressed support for jihadist violence in Syria
In December 2024, al-Shamie had expressed his envy of the mujahideen in Syria and Bashir responded that he had done research about a Salafi-jihadist group operating in Syria called Hayat Tahrir al-Sham (HTS) and expressed a hope that HTS would be able to establish Sharia in Syria.
Bashir also told al-Shamie that he had rejoiced watching videos of Syrian soldiers being beheaded.
Al-Shamie and Bashir had been part of a WhatsApp group called “Aspiring to the Ranks of the Elite” along with 10 others. Bashir sent over 2,000 messages to the chat group in six months. Sentenced judge Justice Cheema-Grubb said many messages were about physical violence in the cause of jihad, including calls for persecution and murder.
“You exalted martyrdom,” she addressed Bashir during the sentencing. “You praised Palestinian fighters, and you posted messages about restoring glory to Muslim lands by exterminating Jewish men and taking Jewish women as concubines. In three posts you sent to the group, you distributed material originating from the well-known American Yemeni cleric who endorsed violence as a religious duty, Anwar al-Awlaki.”
Pair conducted hostile reconnaissance at UK Defense Academy
Aside from the extremist content of the messages, the courts established that both men had made a 10-hour round trip to conduct hostile reconnaissance of the UK Defence Academy in Shrivenham on August 14, 2025. The motive for the proposed attack was that “IDF soldiers had been training there,” the judge said.
Bashir acted as driver for most of the journey, and al-Shamie made notes regarding the security arrangements for the base on his phone, titled “espionage.”
The phone data, alongside CCTV footage and audio showing them discussing plans for an attack, provided the evidence against Bashir.
During sentencing, he was described by the prosecution as a “long-standing, committed jihadist.”
Judge Cheema-Grubb delivered a powerful sentencing with reference to Jewish biblical text.
“Your example must stand as a warning to men who are attracted by this form of militant, violent, and destructive Islam for religious or political reasons,” she said. “In the Jewish book of Proverbs 13:20 this is expressed bluntly, ‘He that walks with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed.’ The values and behavior of the people you revere will directly influence your own life and choices.”
Assistant Chief Constable Rob Potts, who holds operational responsibility for CTPNW, said: “Mohammed Bashir is a dangerous individual with extremist views who held conversations about preparing for ‘war.’
“Bashir is now facing the consequences of his actions, and I hope his sentence will act as a deterrent to anybody considering taking part in extremist activity.”

Tel Aviv protesters block roads, demand Shin Bet investigates Haymanut Kasau's disappearance

Dozens of protesters entered the Ayalon Highway near Tel Aviv’s Azrieli Center on Sunday evening, blocking traffic and calling for the investigation into the disappearance of Haymanut Kasau to be expanded.
Israel Police removed the protesters from the highway. Haymanut’s relatives and activists campaigning on her behalf said the investigation had exhausted the police’s capabilities and demanded that the Shin Bet (Israel Security Agency) take over the case.
Racheli Abata, a campaign activist and relative of Kasau, told Walla that the family viewed the authorities’ handling of the case as an ongoing failure.
“Her case is an abduction. She is defined as missing, but in practice she was abducted on February 25, 2024, from the absorption center," said Abata. "The family is now asking for the Shin Bet to enter the investigation because they believe the police have failed.
"Her case was transferred to Lahav 433 - The National Crime Unit, and since then there has been no response," Abata continued. "Her father is furious, and there is no one to provide answers. He knows that only the Shin Bet can help, and he is asking for a new investigative team to be established.”
Abata said the lack of progress had left the family with no choice but to take to the streets.
“There has been a failure here," Abata stressed. "A nine-year-old girl was abducted, and 14 minutes were deleted from the recording. We understand that there is no other way except to block roads and take to the streets. For more than two years, I have been seeing stories of unequal treatment. The sweet boy who went missing in Ashkelon, I shared posts about him constantly, and within a day the Shin Bet joined the investigation. What about Haymanut?
Abata: Shin Bet involvement would make 'enormous difference'
"Look at the enormous difference when the Shin Bet is involved in an investigation," she added. "We receive no updates and do not know where things stand. This is a family that has been in Israel for five years, and she was abducted after just three years. She is seen entering the complex, but she is not seen leaving.”
She said the family believed the authorities had abandoned them and that time was working against Haymanut.
“The police admitted that this was beyond their capabilities. Enough, we have reached our limit. The authorities have abandoned us. Apart from politics, we do not see them doing anything. We do not know what condition she is in, or whether she is eating and drinking. Time is not on her side.”
Abata said the investigation had been marked by repeated failures since Haymanut disappeared.
“What is left for us to do other than fight? We feel that her case is being neglected. Since she was abducted, there has been one failure after another. Everything appeared to have been planned. It is inconceivable that a girl is abducted and there is deafening silence. Bring her back already.”
Haymanut disappeared from an immigrant absorption center in Safed 882 days ago. Her father, Taspia Kasau, said the rapid discovery of four-year-old Yuval Kogan highlighted the disparity between the response to Yuval’s disappearance and the handling of his daughter’s case.
“First of all, I am happy and moved that Yuval was found quickly, and that his family was spared many more tears,” he told Walla. “But the gap is heartbreaking. All the organization surrounding his disappearance, and the entire system that mobilized to search for and find him, only highlights what was not done for Haymanut.”
Kasau said the family had made the same demand for more than two and a half years, calling on the state to use its advanced technological capabilities to locate Haymanut.
“For two and a half years, we have been saying that the state has advanced technological capabilities to find any person. We held protests, met with people, including Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, and asked them to use those capabilities. None of that happened, and our girl is still not with us.”
He said the family could not ignore its sense that discrimination had influenced the handling of the case.
“I have no doubt at all that this is based on a racist attitude. We are new immigrants, we do not speak Hebrew, we are not powerful, and we have no connections or money. By contrast, this child came from a family that knew how to mobilize all the resources, connections, and forces, and he was found within about 24 hours. It only highlighted what we have always felt, that this is connected to the fact that we are Ethiopian and do not speak Hebrew.”
One of the family’s main demands is for the Shin Bet to join the investigation.
“In Yuval’s case, both the Shin Bet and Military Intelligence were involved. In our case, the Shin Bet did not become involved at all. If they had been involved, we would have known,” he said.
Kasau said that although the attorney general approved the possibility of involving the Shin Bet in the investigation in January, the agency had not joined the case.
“We were told that no nationalistic motive was suspected, and therefore the Shin Bet did not become involved.”
He added that security professionals had told the family that Haymanut’s disappearance from a public institution operated by the Jewish Agency and the Aliyah and Integration Ministry justified the agency’s involvement.
'An attack on an entity of the state'
“This is an attack on an entity of the state,” he said.
Kasau said the family’s meeting with Netanyahu had also failed to produce any change.
“He told us he would examine the matter and get back to us with an answer. We have not heard from him since.”
He also criticized the police’s handling of the investigation, saying the family had received little information about its progress.
“I do not even know where things stand today. At Lahav 443, they do not answer my calls. The police in Safed did not do what they needed to do. Even when we brought them information, they did not really examine it. Only after members of Knesset raised the issue in a Knesset committee did they go and investigate.”
Kasau stressed that his criticism was directed at the state and its institutions, rather than the Israeli public.
“My anger is directed at the state, the government, and the authorities, not at the citizens. The citizens are the ones showing us the way through the darkness. They help us, march with us, and strengthen us. I can see on their faces that their hearts are broken.”
He called on the public to continue supporting the family’s campaign and to urge the government to bring the Shin Bet into the investigation.
“Stand by us and help us call on the state to bring the Shin Bet into Haymanut’s case. I am asking people to pressure the government to do this. I believe that the girl was abducted, that she was taken and is being held by people. She must be searched for using every available means.”
Kasau said he struggled to describe the reality his family had faced since his daughter disappeared.
“It is impossible even to express in words what it feels like when your son or daughter is taken from you, and you do not know where they are. You cannot sleep, you cannot eat, and you cannot speak. I know that this is what Yuval’s parents felt while he was missing. But for us, this has already continued for 882 days. I feel as though I am between the living and the dead. My younger children also constantly ask why she was taken and where she is. When I walk down the street and see little girls her age, I am constantly searching. I constantly think that perhaps I will find her. I would not wish this experience on anyone.”
He said Haymanut deserved the same treatment as any other Israeli citizen.
“This country belongs to all of us. It is a country for all Jews, regardless of the color of our skin. I expect the public to demand that the state give Haymanut the same treatment. There is no fundamental difference between them. We are all citizens of the same country.”
Haymanut’s family said its campaign would not end with a single protest and would continue to press the authorities to expand the investigation.
