Jeremy Clarkson shares the ‘unpleasant’ reality of working on a farm
- Jeremy Clarkson says he’ll work between 15 and 20 hours a day on Diddly Squat in the summer, doing “unpleasant” jobs, yet claims it would be "cheaper to go on an expensive holiday" than to profit from his harvest.
- He has consistently highlighted the financial difficulties of farming, previously revealing it's "impossible" to make money from growing food and leaving 200 acres unplanted due to unprofitability.
- Despite the lack of financial reward, Clarkson expresses deep satisfaction and enjoyment from the physical act of harvesting, describing it as an "absolute joy".
- Clarkson has become an active advocate for agricultural policy, challenging government planning reforms and protesting against proposed increases to inheritance tax on farm assets.
- The Independent has contacted the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs for comment regarding Clarkson's claims about government policy and farming profitability.

Boy George parts ways with record label manager Tony Pontius
- Boy George has announced he has parted ways with his record label manager, Tony Pontius, after Pontius refused to be involved with the singer's new pro-Israel track.
- The Culture Club singer released a song titled We Will Dance Again, which defends Israel's military actions in Palestine and disputes claims of genocide in Gaza.
- Boy George said that his decision to “stand up for [his] Jewish friends” has led to him losing people from his life, including his manager, whom he described as “gutless”.
- The controversial song's lyrics dismiss the idea of genocide, stating, “You say genocide, I say war,” and appear to criticise the pro-Palestine movement.
- The singer previously withdrew from his role as King Herod in Jesus Christ Superstar due to the controversy and explained he was inspired to write the song after meeting families of victims from the Nova music festival attack.
