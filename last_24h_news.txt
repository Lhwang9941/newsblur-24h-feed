President Volodymyr Zelensky has denied allegations of drug use, based on comments spread by US conservative bloggers and his former press secretary, addressing the claims during an interview in Kyiv with US commentator Laura Loomer.
In response to Loomer’s questions regarding Russian propaganda and statements made by prominent influencers accusing the president of using narcotics, Zelensky described the individuals spreading the claims as “crazy people.”

During the interview, Zelensky specifically denied allegations that he used cocaine with French President Emmanuel Macron.
Loomer also referenced claims made by former Fox News host Tucker Carlson, political activist Candace Owens, and a podcast hosted by Zelensky’s former press secretary, Yuliia Mendel. Zelensky rejected Mendel’s allegations and stated that he does not watch Carlson’s content.
“There are different people and different TV presenters, bloggers, and so on. Some of them are good, others are too harsh,” Zelensky said. “But there are smart people and there are unintelligent people. As for me, I watch smart people, and I never watch unintelligent people because it does not help. Therefore, I have not watched Tucker Carlson’s interviews with anyone.”
Diplomatic efforts and peace negotiations
During the same interview with Loomer, Zelensky discussed the ongoing war, stating he is ready to sign a peace agreement with Russia wherever US President Donald Trump chooses, including at the White House Oval Office or his Mar-a-Lago residence.

“Yes, I’m ready. I’ve always told President Trump that I’m ready to come to the US, to the Oval Office or to Mar-a-Lago. It depends on President Trump,” Zelensky said, adding that the real issue is not the venue, but when such an agreement can be reached.
Zelensky stated that saving Ukrainian lives remains his overriding priority. While Ukraine intends to prevail against Russia, he noted that ending the fighting sooner would be preferable to continuing the war for another decade or two at the cost of more casualties.
His remarks align with ongoing efforts by Washington and Kyiv to revive diplomatic initiatives. Zelensky spoke this week with Trump envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner about intensifying diplomatic engagement with Moscow. Furthermore, US and Ukrainian officials are discussing a possible air ceasefire that could form part of a renewed proposal to Russia.
The diplomatic push could accelerate in the coming days, as Trump and Zelensky are expected to meet in Washington.
Zelensky told Loomer that a private conversation with Trump at the Vatican in April 2025 served as a turning point in their relationship, easing tensions that had built up following a confrontation in the Oval Office in February 2025. According to Zelensky, the US president came to understand that Ukraine wanted to end the war, while Russia remained unwilling to halt the fighting.
Earlier, Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov claimed the US still has unfulfilled “obligations” stemming from the August 2025 summit between Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Anchorage, Alaska. Speaking after talks with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio in Manila, Lavrov pointed to proposals discussed in Anchorage as a basis for further negotiations.
Rubio stated that the territorial proposals discussed during the Anchorage summit had failed because Ukraine did not accept them, referring to what Russia described as the “3+2” approach. Rubio stressed that no peace agreement could work without the consent of both sides and noted that the approach was likely even less acceptable to Kyiv today, necessitating new proposals.

Europe wildfires latest: Bordeaux suburbs evacuated as France sends in army while winds in Spain could worsen blaze
Officials have described it as the ‘worst fire in the history of the region’

Over 200,000 people have been forced to evacuate their homes as massive wildfires continue to rage across southwestern France and central Spain.
On Saturday officials ordered the evacuation of large parts of Bordeaux’s suburbs, including the areas of Le Haillan, Eysines and Merignac.
Bordeaux is the largest city in the southwestern region and France’s sixth-largest city overall.
“The fires which are hitting our country have reached a level that has never been seen before,” French prime minister Sebastien Lecornu wrote on X on Saturday.
The French army has also been drafted in to help with the crisis as the blaze showed no signs of slowing down.
Spanish and French officials warned that winds were likely to exacerbate the fires.
Madrid's regional president Isabel Díaz Ayuso said that it marked the “worst fire in the history of the region” and that conditions had been made worse by a combination of high temperatures, wind and the merging of blazes.
The city’s emergency management chief Carlos Novillo told reporters earlier Friday that the fire was “at its peak and... currently beyond the capacity of firefighters to contain”.

Massive forest fires raging across southwestern France and central Spain have forced more than 200,000 people from their homes.
A blaze near Cap Ferret, located just 37 miles from Bordeaux on France's Atlantic coast, has consumed some 48 square miles since Wednesday and has placed the this area, known for its stunning landscapes and upmarket resorts, under severe threat.
In Spain, the government declared a national emergency for wildfires for the first time as blazes in Madrid and the nearby province of Ávila prompted the evacuation from towns west of the capital.
Dozens of firefighters have been injured, authorities said, but no deaths were reported in either France or Spain.
