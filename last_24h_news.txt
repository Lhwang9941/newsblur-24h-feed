No lessons have been learned from last July’s attempts to limit the powers of anti-corruption bodies, those who falsified cases against detectives remain in their posts, and pressure is being put on judges hearing detectives’ cases, heads of Ukraine’s anti-corruption bodies said.
"The witness in [detective Mahamedrasulov’s] case has been acquitted, and according to our information, pressure is being put on the judges who issued the acquittal," National Anti-Corruption Bureau (NABU) Director Semen Kryvonos said at a briefing in Kyiv on Monday.
The detective himself is currently reviewing the investigative case materials, Kryvonos said.
Specialized Anti-Corruption Prosecutor’s Office (SAPO) Head Oleksandr Klymenko said: "This, by the way, is evidence that the consequences of last July’s events still remain: a huge number of detectives are under investigation, quite unreasonably in our view, and no measures are being taken."
Those involved in falsifying these investigations against detectives still remain in their posts, the SAPO head said.
"No consequences, no lessons have been learned from this event," Klymenko said.
Detectives under investigation are turning to judges for support, the NABU director said.
"We constantly record calls from certain departments of the Security Service of Ukraine and pressure on them," he said, adding that judges do not file complaints about the pressure with the relevant bodies.
"This all looks designed to prevent NABU detectives from defending themselves by any procedural means," Kryvonos said.

How Lebanon responded to Israeli map claiming parts of its south
When an official Israeli Foreign Ministry account published a map that appeared to redraw Lebanon’s southern border, officials in Beirut did not respond publicly. Rather, they quickly moved behind the scenes.
The map, posted on the Israeli ministry’s Arabic and English-language accounts on X, appeared to incorporate parts of southern Lebanon into Israel.
It was eventually deleted, but not before setting off concern among Lebanese officials that what Israel would later describe as merely "an artificial intelligence error" could point to territorial ambitions at a time when Israeli troops continue to occupy Lebanese land.
According to information obtained by The New Arab, President Joseph Aoun instructed his team to investigate the post soon after it appeared and to take steps to have it removed.
Aoun considered the map a serious infringement on Lebanon’s sovereignty and its internationally recognised borders, and according to the sources, his team sent reports and geographic coordinates documenting Lebanon’s boundaries.
The matter was then taken up in Washington.
Lebanon’s ambassador to the United States, Nada Hamadeh Moawad, raised the issue with the US State Department, which in turn contacted Israeli officials seeking an explanation.
The Israeli response was that an alleged "error" had occurred while the graphic was being produced using artificial intelligence.
The post was subsequently removed.
But in Beirut, its deletion did little to settle the matter.
According to the sources, Lebanese officials remain concerned that the image could reflect broader Israeli ambitions over southern Lebanon, particularly as Israel continues military operations in the country and has resisted Lebanese demands for a full withdrawal from occupied territory.
The issue is now expected to be raised in the next round of negotiations between Lebanon and Israel, should they proceed.
For Beirut, the episode is also likely to reinforce its insistence that Israel seeks delays over its withdrawal, new pilot areas, or any halt to its military operations in Lebanon.
Lebanon is also weighing whether to continue in the current process without changes, including suspending talks altogether or make another round conditional on Israel taking tangible steps to fulfil its commitments.
The seventh round of talks, held over three days in Rome last week, ended without progress on two of the central disputes between the sides: the establishment of further pilot zones and continued Israeli military operations.
A deeper fear
The disputed image was ostensibly about something entirely different.
It appeared in an infographic presenting figures attributed to the UN children's agency UNICEF on acute child malnutrition across the Middle East, with countries shaded in different colours alongside statistical data.
But Lebanon's borders were altered. Parts of its south were visually absorbed into the territory marked as Israel.
That detail drew particular attention because of what is already happening on the ground.
Israel continues to occupy Lebanese territory while carrying out strikes and other military attacks in the south, despite ongoing negotiations intended in part to establish a path towards withdrawal.
The combination of the map and those developments prompted fears among some Lebanese politicians that the image should not be treated simply as a badly generated graphic.
Hezbollah warned on Sunday that the map provided "confirmation beyond doubt" of Israel's ambitions over Lebanon's territory, wealth and resources.
The group said that Israel's participation in direct negotiations with the Lebanese authorities was an attempt to buy time and "impose new realities on the ground".
It also sharply criticised the Lebanese government's approach to the negotiations, saying it was pursuing concessions without securing reciprocal Israeli steps.
"The Lebanese authorities bear full responsibility for insisting on continuing down the path of concessions and offering free gifts to the enemy," Hezbollah said in a statement.
The group called for a comprehensive reassessment of the negotiations and urged the government and Higher Defence Council to take immediate action, including filing an urgent complaint with the UN Security Council.
It said Lebanese political forces should recognise Israeli ambitions were not limited to the country's south, but extended to Lebanon as a whole.
'Not an incidental detail'
MP Melhem Khalaf also rejected the suggestion that the map could simply be brushed aside as merely a technical mistake.
In a post on X, Khalaf noted that the image was also published by the Israeli Foreign Ministry's verified Arabic-language account, giving its depiction of Lebanon particular political significance.
Publishing a map that showed Lebanon "missing parts of its south", with those areas visually incorporated into Israel, was "not an incidental detail nor a technical error that can be ignored", he said.
He highlighted Israel's continued occupation of Lebanese territory, the demolition of homes, uprooting of olive trees, destruction of water wells and other property, and restrictions preventing some residents from returning to their villages.
The publication of the map alongside those actions, he said, had raised "serious concerns about an attempt to change the features of the land and impose an occupation reality by force".
"These acts are not security measures," Khalaf said. "They are serious violations of international humanitarian law and a direct attack on Lebanon's sovereignty and the rights of its people."
He called on the Lebanese government to formally document the episode and immediately raise it with the United Nations and other international bodies, seeking an explicit acknowledgement that the map was invalid and carried no legal or sovereign effect.
Khalaf also called on the United Nations and UNIFIL, the peacekeeping force deployed in southern Lebanon, to protect civilians and their property and prevent the entrenchment of an occupation on Lebanese territory.

iPhone 18 Pro could face shortages amid RAM crisis, here’s what to know
The company is reportedly scrambling to procure more memory chips ahead of its biggest launch of the year
- Bookmark
Next month, Apple is expected to host its September spectacle and, with it, the launch of the tech giant’s next iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max. As always, the rumour mill has gone into overdrive, meaning we already have a pretty good idea of what to expect from the upcoming handsets.
But the RAM crisis continues to wreak havoc on the tech industry, and insiders are suggesting it could lead to shortages for the iPhone 18 Pro. Expected to feature the best camera on any iPhone, it could boast a variable aperture, similar to a DSLR camra.
With no regular iPhone 18 reportedly on the way this year, all eyes are on the iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max and the long-rumoured iPhone Ultra. Here's everything we know so far.
Read more: Best refurbished iPhone deals
Latest iPhone 18 Pro news and rumours
If the latest rumours are to be believed, the iPhone 18 Pro could be harder to acquire than normal come launch day. Former Bloomberg reporter Tim Culpan claims that Apple and its suppliers are scrambling to procure enough memory chips for its upcoming iPhones.
Culpan reports that “assemblers are working with Apple to rush shipments of DRAM used in mobile devices”, adding that Apple relies heavily on chip manufacturer Micron for DRAM, but also procures from SK Hynix and Samsung.
That could mean the iPhone 18 Pro will be in even shorter supply than normal when it launches in September, especially if the tech giant isn’t able to manufacture as many smartphones as it would like. Six weeks out and production should ordinarily be full steam ahead by now, with Apple building up stock ahead of one of its biggest launches of the year.
Apple iPhone 18 Pro release date
The release timeline for the iPhone 18 and subsequent iPhones could look a lot different going forward. In late 2025, the ever-reliable Apple insider Mark Gurman claimed that the company will be moving away from its annual autumnal launch extravaganza. Instead, Gurman claimed it will be spreading its iPhone launches throughout the year.
According to Gurman, the reason for the shift is that Apple’s decision to cram everything into September has become burdensome. It gives the company fewer opportunities to spread out revenue across the year, while also straining its marketing and engineering teams and overloading suppliers.
So Apple’s predicted to shake up its launch window entirely. Gurman says we won’t be seeing the regular iPhone 18 unveiled in September this year. Instead, Apple will only launch the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max, as well as its first foldable phone.
The tech giant is then predicted to launch the regular iPhone 18 in the spring of 2027, alongside the iPhone 18e. There’s precedent for this – we’ve just seen Apple launch the iPhone 17e in March, for example. Gurman says the brand could also launch a follow-up to the iPhone Air.
As for the actual release date of the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max, that’s still fairly easy to predict, given that the date of Apple’s September spectacle isn’t expected to change.
Apple almost always announces its new line of smartphones in the second week of September. To give you an idea, the iPhone 17 was announced on Tuesday 9 September 2025, the iPhone 16 was announced on Monday 9 September 2024, the iPhone 15 on Tuesday 5 September 2023, the iPhone 14 on Wednesday 7 September 2022 and the iPhone 13 on Tuesday 14 September 2021.
Labor Day in the US is on Monday 7 September this year, so Apple is unlikely to unveil its latest launches then. I’m expecting Apple to show off the iPhone 18 Pro on either Tuesday 8 September or Wednesday 9 September – most likely the latter, with pre-orders going live on Friday 11 September. Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman reported in late June that he also believed the iPhone 18 Pro would be unveiled on one of those dates.
The iPhone 18 Pro could then be released on Friday 18 September.
Apple iPhone 18 Pro price
While I always take price increases with a grain of salt, when it comes from Tim Cook himself, it’s no longer just a rumour.
On 17 June, in an interview with The Wall Street Journal, Cook warned that a price hike would be unavoidable. “We're doing our best to mitigate the huge increases that are being passed to us, and we've been trying to shield our customers from the increases, but the situation has become unsustainable,” he said.
A week later, Apple raised prices of the iPad, MacBook, HomePod and Apple TV in the UK. Apple’s entire range of iPads increased by up to £200, the MacBook by up to £300, the HomePod by up to £50 and the Apple TV 4K by up to £100. It’s not a good sign for the iPhone 18 Pro and 18 Pro Max, which are both expected to house even more RAM to power Apple Intelligence
Analysts are now predicting a price hike for the upcoming iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max. A recent Financial Times report is claiming that Apple is racing to secure more RAM from two Chinese chip manufacturers – ChangXin Memory Technologies and Yangtze Memory Technologies. The report claims Apple is lobbying the US government to allow the company to buy the chips from the two state-backed companies, who are blacklisted by the Pentagon, with the Chinese-made phones potentially being limited to China.
However, it may be too late to help lower the cost of the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max, according to Apple analysts Ming-Chi Kuo, the DRAM shortage will continue to widen into 2027.
Samsung notably increased the price of the Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra earlier this year by £30. Apple’s price hike could be even steeper. Analytics firm IDC expects Apple to increase the iPhone 18 Pro and 18 Pro Max by $100 in the US. Given devices in the UK received the same nominal increase as the US last week, that would translate to a £100 price hike.
The iPhone 17 Pro cost £1,099 at launch, while the iPhone 17 Pro Max launched for £1,199.
This contrasts with what analysts have been saying all year, however, with analysts previously suggesting Apple will absorb any extra costs. Jeff Pu of GF Securities previously stated that the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max will cost the same as the iPhone 17 Pro and iPhone 17 Pro Max. In a February 2026 research note obtained by MacRumours, Pu said Apple was looking at keeping the prices the same or similar by focusing on “cost management”.
In the research note, Pu said Apple had been negotiating with Samsung and SK Hynix to achieve relatively “favourable” memory chip deals, and was looking to lower the costs of other parts, such as the camera and display.
The pricing prediction was first raised by the ever-reliable Apple analyst Ming-Chi Kuo, who said in January that Apple was looking to keep the price of the iPhone 18 Pro the same as the previous generation.
On 12 May, MacRumors published a report claiming that Pu expected Apple to outperform in the smartphone market with an “aggressive pricing strategy” for the iPhone 18 Pro. Pu previously predicted that the starting prices of the iPhone 18 Pro models would either remain unchanged from the iPhone 17 Pro lineup or only increase slightly.
That prediction has seemingly changed in recent weeks. The latest report from Pu has poured a bucket of ice-cold water on any hopes of an affordable iPhone this September.
He now reports that the iPhone 18 Pro could come with a $250-$300 price hike, which translates to roughly £185-£220 in the UK. If true, that could mean the iPhone 18 Pro starts from £1,299, making it the most expensive iPhone Pro ever.
Pu claims this is due to the rising cost of 2nm silicon and DRAM/NAND, with Apple passing those costs on to consumers.
In late July, it was revealed that Apple is reportedly racing to secure cheaper components in a bid to offset the price of RAM, which could help keep down the cost of the next iPhone. Korean-based outlet The Elec reported that Apple is pushing Samsung Display and LG Display to supply its OLED panels for $70 each, but that it could get it even lower than that.
The Elec says Apple may get its new OLED panels for just $66.50, which would be a 20 percent reduction compared to the price paid for the iPhone 17 Pro Max.
While The Elec says that the cost of displays has gone down every year, the reduction Apple is seeking is unusually steep. By bringing down the cost of other components, Apple might be able to produce its next iPhone more cheaply, meaning the price hike to you could also be less.
Apple iPhone 18 design and display
While the iPhone 17 Pro looked dramatically different to the iPhone 16 Pro, leakers right now are predicting the iPhone 18 Pro will look fairly similar to the iPhone 17 Pro. The biggest design change is that the Dynamic Island will be shrunk slightly, so that it doesn’t take up as much screen real estate.
In January this year, leaker Ice Universe claimed the Dynamic Island will be 35 per cent narrower than the current version, with a width of around 13.5mm, down from 20.7mm. The leaker shared a mock-up of what that could look like. Ice Universe’s track record has been fairly good for Samsung leaks, but a bit mixed when it comes to Apple.
Earlier rumours suggested Apple was testing a smaller hole-punch cutout instead of a Dynamic Island, with Face ID moving under the screen. This would give the iPhone a screen without interruptions for the first time. However, in January 2026, Weibo leaker Instant Digital stated this had been a mistranslation, and that there would be no hole-punch cutout instead of a Dynamic Island or under-the-screen Face ID.
Instead, the Face ID flood illuminator is the only component that will be moved under the screen, because it’s relatively small. Display industry analyst Ross Young claims Apple will keep this smaller Dynamic Island until at least 2027.
Several leakers have since backed up Ice Universe’s claim. In late March, X account Early Apple shared a supposed prototype image of the iPhone 18 Pro with a shrunken Dynamic Island. The camera flash also appears to illuminate a faint circular area on the display, hinting at the Face ID flood illuminator being hidden under the screen.
Ice Universe also claimed in late March that the bezels on the iPhone 18 series will remain the same as the iPhone 17 and iPhone 16, while reiterating that the Dynamic Island will be smaller.
Another interesting rumour from Instant Digital is that the iPhone 18 Pro Max could be slightly thicker than the iPhone 17 Pro Max, and could weigh roughly 243g. If true, it would make it the heaviest iPhone Apple’s ever produced. That’s 10g heavier than the iPhone 17 Pro Max and 3g heavier than the iPhone 14 Pro Max (the current heavyweight record holder).
Other than that, the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max could look similar to the previous models, boasting 6.3in and 6.9in displays as well as a triple-lens system with the plateau design introduced on the iPhone 17 Pro.
On 22 April, tech YouTuber Vadim Yuryev shared images of dummy units of the upcoming iPhone 18 Pro Max. And if accurate, it doesn’t look like it’s going to be very different to the iPhone 17 Pro Max. But look a little harder, and you’ll notice that the cameras are a lot thicker, with Yuryev stating the black glass on the cameras protrudes a lot more too.
Why is this? According to long-held rumours, the camera is going to get a massive upgrade in September, with Apple implementing variable aperture for the first time and improved sensors.
The first iPhone 18 Pro dummy unit surfaced in late May and early June, giving us a better look at the dark cherry iPhone colourway. While these are just dummy units, they line up with the predicted colour scheme, and come courtesy of TechDroider Clips on YouTube and a Weibo leaker.
In early May, South Korean publication The Elec reported that the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max could boast advanced LTPO+ OLED panels supplied by Samsung Display and LG Display. The upgraded screens are said to be more power efficient, which could help boost battery life, while also being less susceptible to burn-in.
On 29 June, Reuters revealed that more than 200,000 files stolen from Apple's manufacturing partner Tata Electronics had been leaked on the dark web. The leaked archive included supplier lists, engineering documents, component specifications and photos and videos showing the unreleased iPhone 18 Pro undergoing drop testing. It could be the biggest Apple supply chain breach to date.
Cybersecurity researchers say the stolen data began appearing on the dark web on 10 June, but Tata Electronics hadn’t reported the data breach until 22 June. In the leaked videos and images, a drop test of a smartphone with a three-camera layout and Apple logo can be seen. Last week, AppleInsider reported that board layouts for the iPhone 18 Pro and 18 Pro Max were also contained in the data breach, as well as a data sheet for the upcoming A18 Pro chip.
Apple iPhone 18 Pro colours
One interesting decision Apple made for the iPhone 17 Pro was to give the handsets a two-tone design. Controversially, the glass back for MagSafe charging is a different colour to the aluminium frame, making it look less uniform. Apple is reportedly looking to reduce this contrast for the iPhone 18 Pro.
In November 2025, Instant Digital reported that Apple had changed the phone’s back glass process to help minimise the two-tone look seen on the iPhone 17 Pro, giving the phones a more unified look.
In late February, Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman also claimed that Apple was testing a deep red colour for the upcoming iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone Fold. On 11 April, Weibo leaker Digital Chat Station claimed the same, based on the fact the insider has seen prototypes of Android phones in the same deep red colour.
On 1 May, Macworld claimed a source familiar with Apple’s supply chain had information regarding the colourways for the upcoming smartphones. According to the publication, the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max will come in a dark cherry colourway, which it says will be “much closer to wine than fruit punch”. It says that Apple has also been experimenting with a light blue colourway, similar to the iPhone 17’s mist blue, as well as a dark grey. There will also reportedly be a silver colourway to complete the line-up.
Apple iPhone 18 Pro cameras
Of all the features to receive the biggest shake-up, it’s almost always the camera system
In December 2024, Ming-Chi Kuo claimed the main rear camera on the iPhone 18 Pro will feature variable aperture for the first time, making it act more like a DSLR camera. Variable aperture would enable iPhone users to control how much light comes through the lens. Kuo claimed Dutch manufacturer BE Semiconductor would supply the assembly equipment used to produce the aperture blades.
Several reputable leakers have since corroborated this leak in recent months, with ETNews the latest to do so, in October 2025. The iPhones have had fixed apertures for years, so this could be a significant upgrade.
Apple is also reportedly testing a new telephoto camera for the iPhone 18 Pro. Weibo leaker Digital Chat Station claimed in October last year that the telephoto lens will also feature a larger aperture, being upgraded from the iPhone 17 Pro’s f/2.8 telephoto lens.
Another (fairly old) leak, from January 2025, comes from Taiwanese publication DigiTimes. The publication claimed that Samsung has developed a new three-layer stacked camera sensor system specifically for the iPhone 18. According to DigiTimes, this would make the iPhone 18’s cameras more responsive and reduce noise.
Apple iPhone 18 Pro specs
Spec details for the iPhone 18 Pro are still fairly thin on the ground, but several leaks suggest Apple’s next-generation A20 chip will power the devices.
According to Jeff Pu, the A20 chip is expected to use TSMC’s new 2nm manufacturing process, which could make it up to 15 per cent faster and 30 per cent more efficient than the current A19 chip.
An August 2025 report from Ming-Chi Kuo also suggests the A20 could use a new chip packaging method that integrates RAM directly with the processor, potentially improving performance for Apple Intelligence features, while freeing up space inside the phone.
Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman also claims iPhone 18 Pro models will feature Apple’s next-generation C2 modem. The chip is expected to improve 5G performance and could add support for mmWave connectivity, bringing it closer to the Qualcomm modems Apple currently uses. Weibo leaker Fixed Digital also suggested, in February 2026, that it could support new satellite connectivity standards, potentially allowing for full internet access over satellite networks when traditional coverage isn’t available.
Apple iPhone 18 Pro battery
In early July, new regulatory certification filings from China were discovered by Weibo leaker Digital Chat Station purporting to show the battery capacity for both the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max.
According to the filings in China's C3 database, the iPhone 18 Pro Max will have a 4,056mAh battery in China, while the US eSIM-only handset will get a 4,288mAh battery. That’s slightly up on the 3,988mAh and 4,252mAh battery capacity for the iPhone 17 Pro. The iPhone 18 Pro Max will reportedly getting a much bigger battery life boost, however.
The filing says the iPhone 18 Pro Max will boast a 5,391mAh battery in China, with the eSIM-only model in the US getting a 5,567mAh battery. That’s a huge jump on the iPhone 17 Pro Max’s 4,823mAh battery in China and the 5,088mAh battery in the US. In comparison, the Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra has a 5,000mAh battery.
