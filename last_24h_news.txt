Jared Leto’s unsettling onstage comment about ‘high school girls’ resurfaces after new sex abuse allegations
The Oscar-winning actor has said in a statement that he has 'never sexually assaulted anyone in my entire life'
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
Footage of Jared Leto joking about “high school girls” has resurfaced after four women accused him of sexual misconduct while they were teenagers.
The allegations of sexual assault, statutory rape, grooming and threats of sexual assault are made in a new BBC documentary, Jared Leto: Hollywood’s Dark Secret.
The women interviewed in the film said they encountered the 54-year-old Oscar-winning actor and musician between 2002 and 2016, when they were each between 16 and 18 years old and he was in his 30s and 40s.
Leto has denied the allegations. In a statement to The Independent, he said: “I have never sexually assaulted anyone in my entire life. These claims are absolutely and categorically false.”
The BBC film also includes a clip of Leto onstage with his band 30 Seconds to Mars at Ex’Pression College in Emeryville, California in 2013.
In the resurfaced footage, Leto can be seen inviting a young woman to join him onstage before saying: “Give me a hug, first of all,” and asking her name and where she is from.
After she told Leto that she was from Los Angeles, he impersonated Matthew McConaughey while quoting a line from the 1993 movie Dazed and Confused: “That’s what I like about high school girls. I keep getting older, they stay the same age. Alright, alright, alright.”
Leto first rose to prominence in the 1990s on the television drama My So-Called Life before winning the Academy Award for best supporting actor in 2014 for Dallas Buyers Club. He is also the lead vocalist of Thirty Seconds to Mars, the band he formed with his brother, Shannon, in 1998.
The most serious allegation in the new documentary comes from a woman using the pseudonym Isabel, who alleged Leto sexually assaulted her in a Las Vegas motel bathroom in 2002 when she was 17. “Most of my life I had viewed this event as, I was a stupid girl … and so this bad thing happened,” she told the BBC.
A second woman, identified as Alex, has alleged that Leto threatened her with sexual assault after inviting her to his hotel room following a Thirty Seconds to Mars concert in London in 2013. Alex, who said she was 19 but told Leto she was 17, alleged he replied: “Age is just a number, and anyway, we’re in Europe.”
She said she left after he repeatedly told her she would wake up “with a d*** in her a**”.
Another woman, using the pseudonym Clara, alleged she first had sex with Leto at his California home in 2006 when she was 17 and he was 34. She alleged Leto dismissed a discussion about California's age of consent – which is 18 – and referred to her as “my little girl” while asking her to call him “daddy”.
A fourth woman, identified as Etta, alleged Leto groomed her after meeting her at a Los Angeles modeling agency in 2014, when she was 16. She told the BBC he later made repeated sexually explicit phone calls, asking whether she was a virgin and suggesting they should have sex.
Etta also alleged Leto later attempted to have her sign a non-disclosure agreement. The BBC said it had reviewed the unsigned agreement, along with related emails and text messages.
The investigation also includes accounts from five women who alleged they received sexually explicit phone calls from Leto while they were younger, four of whom said they were teenagers at the time.
Another woman, identified as Taylor, alleged that Leto made a lewd comment about her body and attempted to have a security guard take her backstage after she met him at a music festival when she was 14.
Two former members of Thirty Seconds to Mars’ crew also told the BBC they had been uncomfortable with what they described as Leto’s interactions with teenage girls backstage and at recording locations.
In 2025, an investigation by Air Mail revealed that nine women had accused Leto of sexual impropriety, several alleging they were under the age of consent at the time. The report came weeks after Los Angeles DJ Allie Teilz publicly alleged on social media that Leto assaulted her when she was 17, prompting several other women to come forward with similar claims.
Leto denied any wrongdoing at the time, saying through a representative: “All of the allegations are expressly denied.”

Senators still working with SEC, Big Ten on thorny issues involved in college sports bill
Senate negotiators are working to resolve thorny differences with leaders of the Southeastern and Big Ten Conferences over revisions to a bill designed to regulate college sports that sponsors want a vote on before the chamber goes on summer break next week
- Bookmark
Senate negotiators worked Wednesday to resolve thorny differences with leaders of the Southeastern and Big Ten Conferences over revisions to a bill designed to regulate college sports that sponsors want a vote on before the chamber goes on summer break next week.
“Those conversations are ongoing,” said one of the bill's sponsors, Sen. Ted Cruz, R-Texas.
Cruz and co-sponsor Sen. Maria Cantwell, D-Wash., are pushing to bring the Protect College Sports Act to a vote before Aug. 7, the last day the Senate meets before going on break. Senators return for the second half of September, before breaking for all of October -- leaving little time remaining before the November midterms.
A pair of documents outlining concessions the lawmakers have made in the negotiations were headlined by a $20 million-plus “retention pool” that schools could use to keep players from leaving. It would amount to a doubling of the revenue-sharing “salary cap," which is currently set at $21.3 million for this school year.
The conferences are seeking answers on whether the increase in revenue share would impact rules governing third-party payments to players, the likes of which are largely blamed for spiraling roster costs.
There's also the question of whether such an increase would comport with the House settlement — the $2.8 billion lawsuit settlement that dictates rules around revenue sharing. Jeffrey Kessler, the plaintiffs' attorney, has said he would need to see the language before weighing in on whether it impacts the settlement.
Absent from the documents was any mention of rewrites regarding antitrust protection or preemption of state laws. Those have been core requests surrounding legislation from virtually all the conferences and NCAA since Congress began this process.
Earlier this month, the SEC and Big Ten put out a paper outlining 10 “Common Sense Improvements” for the PCSA. The first two— and three of the 10 — entries on their list dealt with those two issues.
Other topics have been addressed in the negotiations, including adding flexibility to requirements over how many Olympic and women's sports programs some schools should have and relaxing language over conference expansion to allow leagues to grow to up to 19 teams.
There has also been language designed to prohibit private equity investors from paying schools to form a super league, though the conferences could seek more refining to that language.
