Trump mocked over apparent zone-out during Oval Office vaccine announcement
‘This is getting ridiculous,” wrote one X user. Another chimed in: ‘Why doesn't the assembled media speak out about this...they all see it’
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
President Donald Trump appeared to zone out during an Oval Office announcement on vaccines, prompting a fresh round of mockery online.
On Monday, Trump signed an executive order that calls for reducing the number of vaccines recommended for children and splitting the MMR shot — which protects against measles, mumps and rubella — into three individual vaccines.
The order is the latest step in the administration’s campaign to remake the childhood vaccination schedule, led by Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who has long questioned vaccine safety.
During the ceremony, the 80-year-old president appeared to repeatedly close his eyes while seated behind the Resolute Desk — including as Kennedy and National Institutes of Health Director Jay Bhattacharya spoke beside him.
Videos of the moments quickly circulated on social media.
“I love how he pops out of it for a few seconds then closes his eyes again and he's right back in dream land. This is getting ridiculous,” wrote one X user. Another chimed in: “Why doesn't the assembled media speak out about this...they all see it.”
“What a loser,” a third user wrote. “After years of listening to those ‘sleepy joe’ comments…” The remark referred to Trump’s longtime nickname for former President Joe Biden, whom he repeatedly accused of being mentally and physically unfit for office.
Some commenters, however, were more supportive of the president. “In Trump's defense, good Lord, the speaker is boring,” one wrote.
Trump, the oldest person ever inaugurated as president, has faced persistent scrutiny over his health since returning to the White House, including over moments during official events in which he has appeared to close his eyes for extended periods. One such moment came during Lindsey Graham’s funeral last month.
“The president has severe daytime somnolence,” CNN medical analyst Dr. Jonathan Reiner said in May. “He falls asleep very often. He’s fallen asleep in the Oval Office on multiple occasions with people talking to him, in the cabinet room,” he said. “And chronic insomnia is a severe illness. It can result in an increase in risk of dementia, a decrease in cognitive effects in older people.”
The White House, meanwhile, has forcefully rejected such claims.
“President Trump is the sharpest, most accessible, and energetic president in American history and any so-called medical professionals engaging in armchair diagnosis or false speculation for political purposes are clearly breaking the Hippocratic Oath they’ve sworn to,” a White House spokesperson told The Independent at the time.
Trump, for his part, has repeatedly insisted that he is in excellent health. After a physical at Walter Reed in May, the president wrote on Truth Social that “everything” had “checked out PERFECTLY.”
In a YouGov survey conducted last year, 78 percent of respondents said they believed Trump had not been transparent about his physical health.
The Independent has contacted the White House for comment.

Crackdown on vape shops and bookies as Burnham gives councils power to choose what is on their high streets
New prime minister Burnham to tackle Britain’s ‘hollowed-out high streets’ by scrapping Blair’s Aim to Permit directive
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
Councils will be given greater powers to block betting shops and vape stores under government plans to revitalise Britain’s "hollowed-out high streets".
Andy Burnham plans to abolish the so-called "aim to permit" rule, first introduced by Sir Tony Blair, which limits the ability of town halls to refuse licences to gambling premises and venues with round-the-clock slot machines.
Vape shops will also face tighter controls, with new stores required to see planning approval from next year. The legal definition of a vape store will also be tightened to prevent retailers trying to bypass regulations by describing themselves as a "convenience store”.
Similarly, centres which operate 24-hour slot and fruit machines will also require planning consent under the changes.
The measures are part of a package designed to halt the decline of Britain’s high streets - and even make them look better. New expectations on the design of shop fronts will tackle “garish neon signs” and oversized logos, while there will be “practical guidance” for councils on how to make towns more attractive.
Police will be able to shut “dodgy” barbers, vape shops and nail salons for twice as long - up to a year - to allow officers more time to investigate links to organised crime, under proposals first announced by Sir Keir Starmer’s government.
Mr Burnham said the changes would help put local communities “back in control” and bring town centres back to life.
He said: “For many people, the high streets they grew up with have been hollowed out and become unrecognisable over decades of decline.
“The rise of vape shops, betting shops and rogue operators have replaced the shops, services, and community spaces that people are crying out for.
“That’s not on. I said we would improve Britain’s high streets, and that’s exactly what we are starting to do.”
He added: “High streets are the beating heart of local communities, but for too long Westminster has stood by while they have been in decline. This government is committed to restoring our high streets and bringing back hope across Britain.”
The “aim to permit” policy was introduced when the Blair government, in which Mr Burnham was a minister, liberalised gambling laws in 2005.
The prime minister, who has returned from a holiday a little over a fortnight into the job, will spend much of August travelling the UK asking people how the government can improve their lives.
He will have a focus on reviving high streets and tackling the cost of living, while officials say he will be in “listening mode”.
The Centre for Social Justice think tank, which has found that Britain has lost nearly 1,800 pubs and bars while vape and tobacco shops have increased in number to almost 2,200 since 2016, welcomed the plans.
Joshua Nicholson, head of housing and communities at the think tank, said: “The government is right to give councils new powers to say no to these dodgy shops. High street renewal is vital to restoring security within our communities.”
But Sir James Cleverly, the shadow local government secretary, said: “Everyone can see what has happened to our high streets, with once-treasured shops being replaced by a smattering of rogue operators, many of which have links to organised crime and fraud.
“Labour’s business rates hikes have hollowed out our high streets, driving out the shops people want and leaving empty units for whoever is left standing to fill. Conservatives would cut business rates for high street firms and back the small businesses that give a town centre its character.
“Unless the government takes steps to support the high streets and hard-working businesses, Labour’s increases in planning regulation will just mean more empty and boarded up shops.”

WHO warns it is ‘chasing’ Ebola as death toll surpasses 1,900: ‘The virus is ahead of us’
Confirmed cases of Ebola in this outbreak have reached 4,200, including over 1,900 deaths, according to the latest government figures
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
The most rapidly spreading Ebola outbreak ever recorded actually began in February, several months prior to its official declaration in mid-May, according to the World Health Organization, as responders in eastern Congo attempt to catch up.
Confirmed cases of Ebola during this outbreak have now reached 4,200, resulting in more than 1,900 deaths, according to recent government figures.
Speaking at a news conference, the WHO regional director for Africa Dr Mohamed Yakub Janabi explained that genetic sequencing revealed the earlier start.
He noted that initial cases were mistakenly diagnosed as other illnesses, including malaria and typhoid.
"So we are chasing the virus, the virus is ahead of us," Janabi said.
Dr Thierno Balde, WHO's incident manager for the Ebola response, stated that the genetic sequencing was carried out under the leadership of Dr Steve Ahuka, head of the virology department at Congo’s National Institute for Biomedical Research.
The WHO assesses the worldwide risk of spread as low because Ebola is not airborne, making it far harder to transmit than respiratory viruses.
Cases identified outside the affected region have so far been promptly caught and contained without causing sustained transmission, the agency said.
The outbreak declared on May 15 is unlike most previous ones for Ebola because the rare Bundibugyo virus responsible for it has no approved vaccines or treatments. Early testing was conducted for the more common type of Ebola, contributing to delays.
The outbreak is unfolding in some of the most challenging conditions imaginable, with strikes by some unpaid health workers, threats by rebel groups, anger from long-traumatized communities and misinformation asserting that Ebola isn’t real. Health teams travel remote, unpaved roads and have reported shortages of protective gear, while a vast population of displaced people struggles to find reliable water sources for washing hands.
This Ebola outbreak has killed more people at a quicker rate than any other, including the 2014-2016 outbreak in West Africa that is considered the worst on record with over 11,000 deaths out of at least 28,000 cases. That one took about eight months to reach 1,000 deaths.
Ebola is rare but highly contagious and can be contracted from bodily fluids such as vomit, blood or semen and with contaminated surfaces and materials such as bedding and clothing. The disease it causes is severe and often fatal.
Last week, WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesu said Ebola is spreading faster than efforts to fight it. New cases are doubling in some hot spots, Tedros said on X after meeting with officials in the capital, Kinshasa.
Health authorities have said between 60% and 70% of new cases are recorded outside of contacts being monitored, and that the outbreak is spreading at an “alarming” rate.
Access to healthcare in the region already had been challenged by years of rebel conflict in one of Congo’s most remote and vulnerable areas, near the borders of South Sudan, Uganda and Rwanda. Now some pregnant women and others are avoiding health centers for fear of the virus, at risk to their lives.
Several past Ebola outbreaks have also been declared weeks or months late, including the 2014-2016 West Africa outbreak. That outbreak was declared in March 2014, although the first human case later was found to have occurred in December 2013.
