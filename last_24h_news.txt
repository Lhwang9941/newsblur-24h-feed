Suspected Irish crime boss lands in Dublin after being extradited from Dubai
Daniel Kinahan is accused of running an international drug smuggling and money-laundering empire that stretched to the UK, Spain and the US
The alleged leader of an Irish organised crime gang has been extradited to Ireland from Dubai to face charges that he ran an international drug smuggling and money-laundering empire with tentacles that stretched to Britain, Spain and the United States.
Air Tracker data showed an Irish Air Corps plane from Dubai landing on Sunday evening at a military airport on the outskirts of Dublin after courts in the United Arab Emirates refused to block his extradition.
The plane is believed to be carrying Daniel Kinahan, 49, who is expected to be taken directly to the Special Criminal Court in Dublin where he will face charges of directing an organised crime group. He will then be held at the high-security Portlaoise Prison about 145km (90 miles) southwest of the capital, Irish broadcaster RTE reported.
Ireland’s Special Criminal Court handles all organised crime and terrorism cases, as well as others where prosecutors believe the ordinary courts are inadequate to ensure the “effective administration of justice”. Cases are heard by a panel of three judges without a jury.
Irish media report that a huge security operation is under way to ensure Kinahan faces justice. It will include armed police, members of the armed forces and the prison service – which recently acquired a bombproof escort van to transport organised crime suspects, terrorists and other high-risk prisoners, Irish broadcaster RTE reported.
The security precautions come amid allegations that the Kinahans are engaged in a bloody gang war with another Irish organised crime group.
“Since February 2016, the (Kinahan Organised Crime Group) has been involved in a gang war with another group in Ireland and Spain, resulting in numerous murders, including of two innocent bystanders,” the US Treasury Department said in 2022, when it sanctioned six members of the Kinahan gang and three businesses linked to it.

Syrian security forces arrested a nine-member ISIS cell in Khan Arnabah, Quneitra Province, the Syrian Interior Ministry confirmed on Sunday.
Khan Arnabah is located adjacent to the demilitarized zone that runs along the Israel-Syria border.
The cell carried out tasks for ISIS, including collecting weapons and transporting explosive devices and other related materials, as well as other terror activities intended to support ISIS's operations, the Interior Ministry said.
One of those arrested was cell leader Musab Khalil al-Sheikh, who is also known by the alias "al-Sini" ("the Chinese"), and whom Damascus tied to terror operations across several Syrian provinces, including in the Damascus region.
Sheikh had also operated with a cell that was led by Abu Abdulrahman al-Iraqi, the then-southern commander of ISIS who was killed in Daraa in 2022, the Interior Ministry added.
This comes as part of the "ongoing efforts to track down ISIS members and disrupt the group's supply networks," the Interior Ministry said.
Interior Ministry announces arrest of hundreds of ISIS members during Q2 2026
In June, the Interior Ministry announced that security forces had arrested 235 members of ISIS over the previous three months as part of a broad security campaign aimed at dismantling the group’s active cells and preventing attacks intended to destabilize the country.
The ministry said security and intelligence operations conducted across several Syrian provinces led to the discovery and disruption of seven ISIS operations. Those detained included 198 Syrians and 37 foreign nationals, according to figures released by the ministry.
Additional details released by the Interior Ministry showed that 80 suspects were detained in March, 99 in April, and 56 in May. Authorities said the operations dismantled seven ISIS cells in Damascus, Aleppo, Hama, Homs, and Deir ez-Zor, leading to the seizure of 22 explosive devices, 25 weapons, six vehicles, and dozens of electronic devices used by the group.
Also in June, US Central Command (CENTCOM) announced that it killed a senior ISIS terrorist, identified as "Ali Husayn al-'Ulaywi" in an airstrike in northeast Syria.

Israel must prepare its Joshua Generation for the post-Netanyahu era - opinion

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu will be remembered as one of Israel’s most consequential prime ministers.
During his years in office, Israel strengthened its economy, expanded its technological leadership, demonstrated resilience through war and regional instability, and, together with Strategic Affairs Minister Ron Dermer, helped to achieve the historic Abraham Accords, reshaping the Middle East in ways few believed possible.
These accomplishments deserve recognition. But history judges leaders not only by what they build. It also asks whom they prepare to lead after them. That is the question confronting Israel today.
Israel has become a military, technological, and economic powerhouse. Yet as its strategic strength has reached historic heights, its international legitimacy has increasingly come under attack. On university campuses, in international media, and across social media, Israel is too often portrayed as a European colonial project rather than the ancestral homeland of the Jewish people.
This is not simply a public relations problem. It is a strategic diplomatic challenge.
For nearly two decades, the Netanyahu era strengthened Israel’s military, economy, and regional alliances. Yet it did not sufficiently cultivate a new generation of internationally respected Zionist leaders capable of communicating Israel’s story across cultures and languages. Leadership is measured not only by governing well, but by preparing capable successors.
As an Ethiopian-born Israeli and American educated across Africa, Israel, and New York City, my perspective has been shaped by three continents and multiple democratic systems. My academic and professional work has focused on conflict resolution, restorative peace, cross-cultural education, and moral diplomacy.
Those experiences have convinced me that 21st-century diplomacy requires more than negotiations between governments. It requires trusted communicators who understand history, culture, and the concerns of international audiences.
Today, diplomacy is no longer conducted only in embassies. It is contested daily on university campuses, television studios, podcasts, digital platforms, and international forums where public opinion is often formed before governments begin negotiating. Israel possesses extraordinary military leaders, intelligence professionals, economists, scientists, and entrepreneurs.
Yet it has not invested enough in developing globally respected communicators who can explain Zionism confidently while engaging audiences beyond Israel’s borders. Winning wars remains essential. Winning understanding is equally essential.
Rabbi Jonathan Sacks often taught that Moses’s greatest achievement was not simply leading Israel out of Egypt. It was preparing Joshua. That timeless lesson raises an important question for Israel today.
While the Netanyahu era achieved historic successes in security and economic growth, it did not sufficiently cultivate a widely recognized new generation of global Zionist leaders and diplomats prepared to carry that vision forward.
If Netanyahu returns to office, he still has a historic opportunity to lay the foundation for that Joshua Generation by identifying, mentoring, and empowering the next generation of Israeli leaders who can represent the Jewish state with strategic vision, moral confidence, and international credibility.
Civilizations endure because one generation intentionally prepares the next. Strong democracies continually develop new leaders capable of speaking to new generations.
One of Israel’s greatest untapped diplomatic strengths stands in plain sight: the Ethiopian Jewish community. For centuries before modern political Zionism, Ethiopian Jews prayed toward Jerusalem. We preserved Jewish life in Africa despite isolation and hardship.
We did not arrive as colonizers. We returned as one of the world’s oldest Jewish communities to our ancestral homeland.
That history powerfully challenges the claim that Israel is merely a European colonial enterprise. Yet Ethiopian-Israeli voices have too rarely been placed at the center of Israel’s international diplomacy. This is not about symbolism. It is about strategic effectiveness.
When the world asks whether Israel represents only European Jews, Israel should answer not only with historical documents but also with living testimony – from Ethiopian Jews, Mizrahi Jews, Persian Jews, Indian Jews, Yemenite Jews, and many other Jewish communities whose histories demonstrate that the Jewish people have always been diverse, indigenous, and globally dispersed.
Real representation begins not with promises but with meaningful responsibility.
The Joshua Generation - Israel's next steps
Several political parties have recently included Ethiopian-Israeli candidates on their electoral lists. That is encouraging. But representation is not measured simply by appearing on a ballot. It is measured by whether candidates are placed in realistic positions to enter the Knesset, influence national policy, and help shape Israel’s future.
In conversations with rabbis, educators, and community leaders, I have repeatedly heard the same message: the Ethiopian Jewish community is no longer asking for symbolic representation. It seeks meaningful leadership based on merit, experience, and demonstrated ability. This is not the demand of one political party. It is a challenge to Israel’s entire political system.
Any party that sincerely believes in national unity should demonstrate that commitment through genuine leadership opportunities. The next Israeli government should establish a new generation of global Zionist leadership: the Joshua Generation.
It should identify highly qualified Israelis with expertise in diplomacy, international affairs, communication, education, and public policy and prepare them to represent Israel worldwide.
I also offer one strategic recommendation to Israel’s next prime minister. The next government should intentionally broaden Israel’s senior diplomatic leadership by appointing highly qualified Ethiopian-Israeli professionals to strategic international affairs and public diplomacy positions. This recommendation is based not on symbolism but on Israel’s national interest.
At a time when Israel is falsely portrayed as a European colonial project, accomplished Ethiopian-Israeli leaders can provide living evidence that the Jewish people returned to their ancestral homeland from every corner of the world, including Africa.
Netanyahu’s generation secured remarkable achievements, and history should recognize them fairly. But history will also ask whether that generation prepared Israel for what comes next.
Moses needed Joshua. Israel now needs a Joshua Generation leader with strategic vision, international experience, cultural fluency, moral courage, and the ability to defend the Jewish state’s legitimacy with confidence and historical evidence. Military strength will always remain indispensable. But in the 21st century, moral diplomacy is also a national security asset.
The future of Israeli diplomacy should not simply preserve the achievements of the past. It should demonstrate, through the diversity of the Jewish people themselves, that Israel is not a colonial project but the restored homeland of an ancient nation.
If Israel develops that Joshua Generation, it will strengthen not only its democracy but also its ability to tell its story with confidence, integrity, and truth. In an age of misinformation, that may become one of Israel’s greatest strategic victories.
The writer is a former New York City Supreme Court detective, an investigator and educator in conflict resolution and restorative peace, and a moral diplomacy expert. His upcoming book, Moral Diplomacy for a Broken World, is inspired by the late Rabbi Jonathan Sacks.
