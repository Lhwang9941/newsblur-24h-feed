Foreign nurse banned from the job after telling colleagues: ‘I could kill someone here and I would still get a job at home’
Nimmy George first lost her medical registration to work after being suspended in August 2024
- Bookmark
A foreign nurse has been banned from the profession after telling colleagues: “I could kill someone here and I would still get a job at home.”
Nimmy George, a registered nurse working in Cumbria, was already suspended from practising as a nurse by a Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) fitness to practise committee over misconduct and lack of competence.
But the NMC decided she should be struck off the medical register altogether to prevent her from practising in the future, following a further hearing last week.
The council heard Ms George, believed to be originally from India, failed to recognise a dead patient did not require their observation to be taken and told a colleague: “If I disposed of my passport that has my visa attached, and not declare, they would not know, I would have to stay in the country because of no passport.”
Ms George had lost her medical registration to work after being suspended in August 2024 after a series of NMC findings about her work and the standards of knowledge, skill and judgment required to practise without supervision between 2021 and 2022.
The NMC findings included Ms George shouting out: “He is having a stroke!” and, on another occasion, discovering that a patient was deceased, saying in a loud voice: “It’s my observation that the patient in bed five is dead.”
Other findings by the NMC fitness to practise panel included a lack of knowledge about medicines, failing to pay attention to colleagues during handover, failing to take proper medical notes, failing to check medical records, failing to check patients’ identities and mixing up different patients’ medical notes.
In a ruling, the NMC said Ms George had not engaged with the body after her initial suspension or demonstrated any insight into her failings.
The NMC ruling ended: “The panel therefore concluded that the only sanction that would adequately protect the public and serve the public interest was a striking-off order.”
The striking-off order will take effect upon the expiry of the current suspension order on September 25.
The decision will be confirmed to Ms George in writing.

China, India and Pakistan’s dangerous nuclear triangle just became more complicated
New Delhi faces a strategic headache from its main rival’s defensive pact with Turkey and the Saudis – but it may be better news for Beijing
Last week, Pakistan, Turkey and Saudi Arabia signed a pact in Mecca in which they agreed to treat an armed attack on one as an attack on all. The three governments have described it as defensive and not directed at any particular state.
Analysts said its main significance for India lay less in whether it would be invoked, but in how it could alter Pakistan’s strategic position as well as the balance between the three nuclear powers.
“The agreement institutionalises a trilateral framework combining Saudi capital, Turkish defence technology and Pakistan’s strategic geography,” according to Brahma Chellaney, a New Delhi-based strategic analyst.
“Regardless of whether such provisions are ever invoked [the pact’s] existence could nonetheless complicate India’s strategic calculus by introducing additional uncertainty into crisis planning and deterrence,” he wrote in a social media post.

MOSCOW, August 10. /TASS/. More than 130 unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) crews were trained at the Moscow Military District training grounds over the course of a month, the district’s press service reported.
"Instructors trained more than 130 crews for unmanned systems units at the Moscow Military District’s training grounds in July 2026. The training was conducted for crews operating attack unmanned aerial vehicles, both copter and fixed-wing drones, ground-based robotic systems, and the Supercam reconnaissance UAVs," according to the statement.
It noted that during the training, the soldiers honed their skills in flying drones over obstacles on a special FPV training course equipped with structures simulating urban terrain. In addition, special attention was paid to developing skills in in-flight recording control, simulated target tracking and destruction.
The Moscow Military District’s press service specified that after successfully passing their exams, service members from the Moscow Military District’s unmanned systems units will continue to carry out combat missions in the special military operation zone.
