A Jetstar A320 and a Qatar Airways Boeing 777 narrowly avoided colliding on the tarmac at Sydney airport on Sunday, the Australian Transport Safety Bureau was told, it said, as it began an investigation into the incident.
The Jetstar plane was taxiing for take-off to Queensland state's Gold Coast when its flight crew saw the Qatar Airways plane, which was being towed by an aircraft tug, very close on a crossing taxiway, the regulator said.
"Both aircraft were stopped abruptly," the transport safety bureau said in a statement.
A cabin crew member on the Jetstar plane was injured, and the connection between the Qatar Airways plane's nose gear and the aircraft tug was damaged, the regulator said.
Jetstar opens investigation
Jetstar, the budget arm of Australia's national carrier Qantas, said it was investigating the incident, too.
“Our aircraft was following instructions by Air Traffic Control when the pilots were forced to brake firmly after another aircraft came in close proximity," a Jetstar spokesperson said in a statement.
Qatar Airways did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
The incident comes a day after Sydney Airport was hit by widespread delays sparked by government agency Airservices Australia enacting "departure spacing" due to roster problems with air traffic controllers.
Airservices Australia did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The Foreign Ministry issued a heightened caution advisory on Sunday for Israelis in Greece ahead of demonstrations and rallies expected to take place at dozens of locations across the country against the backdrop of the war in Gaza.
Among other recommendations, Israelis were advised to stay away from gatherings, conceal Israeli and Jewish identifiers, and refrain from posting their location on social media in real time.
The protests are expected to take place in Athens, Thessaloniki, on the islands, and at various tourist destinations.
Large-scale protests and public disturbances expected across Greece
Given the expected scale of the protests and the possibility of large gatherings and public disturbances, the Foreign Ministry called on Israelis to exercise increased vigilance and avoid any friction or confrontation with protesters.
Israelis were also asked to follow announcements from local authorities and media outlets, keep mindful of developments, and comply with instructions from local security forces.

Israel's restraint in Gaza may give Hamas the opening it needs to take its power back - analysis

The IDF has achieved significant gains in the Gaza Strip, particularly over the past year, during which it created a well-oiled machine for locating, mapping, and destroying terrorist infrastructure above and below ground.
As a result, the IDF currently controls between 65% and roughly 70% of the Gaza Strip, depending on whom you ask.
At the same time, Southern Command chief Maj.-Gen. Yaniv Asor, who will soon compete for the position of deputy IDF chief of staff, advanced a very clear policy of thwarting terrorism through airstrikes. The strikes are directed at anyone who took part in October 7, with an emphasis on operatives who crossed the border into Israeli territory, as well as those currently advancing terrorist activity against Israel and rebuilding the capabilities of Hamas's military wing.
This involves a combination of precise intelligence from the Shin Bet (Israeli Security Agency), which does not receive proper credit for its achievements, intelligence from the IDF Intelligence Directorate, and aerial firepower capabilities. A senior officer told Walla that "this is high-quality intelligence and the exploitation of operational opportunities."
However, in recent weeks, amid pressure from new Hamas leader Khalil al Hayya, as well as Qatar, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and the United Arab Emirates on the US, there has been a significant shift in the approach toward Israeli activity in the Gaza Strip.
Israel approves task force entry into Gaza before Hamas disarmament
First, the Israeli government gave in to US pressure and approved the entry of approximately 200 members of a multinational force into the Gaza Strip before Hamas had disarmed and transferred responsibility to the technocratic government.
Shortly after criticism emerged from within the defense establishment over the pragmatism the government showed toward US President Donald Trump's peace plan, while ignoring Hamas's ability to rebuild and reestablish itself in the Gaza Strip, particularly its military wing, a decision was made to completely halt targeted killings in the Gaza Strip, without presenting alternatives.
On this issue, criticism can be directed at the Israeli government, which insisted on preventing the Palestinian Authority from becoming involved in the Strip, and, on the other hand, at the policies of Trump, envoy Nikolay Mladenov, and the technocratic government, which are rushing to rehabilitate the southern Gaza Strip.
They have done so without presenting a strong framework capable of preventing Hamas from returning to rule the entire strip, including the area that is supposed to be "terror-free" in the Rafah area. In practice, what is happening is that the US and Israeli policy are allowing actors hostile to Israel, such as Qatar and Turkey, to expand their influence in the Gaza Strip and over Israel.
New Rafah construction by US makes no sense, experts say
The US approach to the establishment of the new city in Rafah is also raising questions among professionals. According to them, the quality of the buildings and infrastructure, and the speed at which the Americans want contractors to complete them, make no sense and are inconsistent with the idea of establishing a municipal system capable of absorbing 50,000 Palestinians who are not involved in terrorism.
As if that were not enough, it was learned that a Nukhba operative who was directly involved in October 7 was arrested near the Kerem Shalom Crossing by the Shin Bet and the IDF after receiving authorization to work as a driver transporting humanitarian aid.
An analysis of the developments surrounding the Gaza Strip suggests that the government's policy in Gaza is beginning to become dangerous and could lead to further negative developments.
