BERLIN, August 9. /TASS/. Europe is waging a hybrid war against Russia by providing Kiev with military assistance and imposing sanctions against Moscow, Serbian President Aleksandar Vucic said.
"You have armed Ukraine and imposed sanctions against Russia. You have also provided financial support to Ukraine - what is this, if not a hybrid war against Russia?" he said in an interview with Axel Springer CEO Mathias Dopfner.
He noted at the same time that he "doesn’t know how Russia can be defeated." "After all, Russia is a nuclear power and no one can just attack it," he said, adding that "a compromise solution rather than the defeat of any of the parties" is needed to end the conflict in Ukraine.

Russia and Ukraine exchange overnight strikes that leave 7 dead, many injured
Ukrainian drones killed 5 people in Russia’s border region of Belgorod while Russian missiles killed 2 in Kharkiv
Russia and Ukraine exchanged strikes overnight that killed civilians and injured many others on both sides, local authorities reported.
Large-scale Russian missile strikes on Ukrainian cities have become almost routine in recent weeks, with Moscow exploiting Ukraine’s critical shortage of interceptors for the US-made Patriot systems – the sole air defence weapon in its arsenal able to shoot down ballistic missiles.
In the latest attacks, Ukrainian drones killed five people and injured two dozen others, including a four-year-old boy, in Russia’s border region of Belgorod, according to the regional administration.
In Kharkiv, Ukraine’s second-largest city, Russian missiles struck a block of flats block in the Saltivskyi district, killing two people and injuring 13, according to regional head Oleh Syniehubov. Eight more people were injured in Ukraine’s Black Sea port of Odesa after “dozens of missiles and drones” struck the city and surrounding region, according to local administration head Oleh Kiper.
Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelensky has for months lobbied other countries, especially the US, to send Ukraine more Patriot systems made scarcer by the Iran war, arguing they are crucial for defending civilians.
Failing that, Zelensky wants to make Patriot ammunition in Ukraine or get approval from Elon Musk to use his Starlink satellite communications system to guide strikes inside Russia that can hit its missile launchers.

Wallabies encouraged to play with freedom after defeating Japan in Osaka
Mon 10 Aug 2026 at 6:04am
In short:
The Wallabies have been given a "licence to play" after winning a frantic and high-tempo Test match against Japan on Saturday.
Australia were scrappy at times during Les Kiss's first match as coach, but will be hoping for an improved performance in the second Test next weekend.
What's next?
ABC Sport will have a live blog of the second Test match between the Wallabies and Japan on Saturday, August 15.
The Wallabies are committing to having a crack after being given a "licence to play" by new coach Les Kiss, having avoided an Osaka disaster.
Australia defied a 20-minute red card and brutal heat and humidity to pip Eddie Jones's plucky Japan 35-32 and ensure the Kiss era began with victory on Saturday.
Fly half Declan Meredith battled all night to make ground with his exit kicks, and the fluency shown in a last-start flogging of Italy was not there.
But, after winning just five of 15 Tests under Joe Schmidt last year, the Wallabies did enough and will meet Japan again in friendlier conditions in Townsville this Saturday afternoon, hoping to find a higher gear.
"We've tweaked a few things, but we didn't even really get to see those out there," halfback Ryan Lonergan said.
Japan's opening try came after Fraser McReight had won a turnover and raced out of his own 22-metre area with a quick tap, only to turn the ball over with a speculative pass.
When the Wallabies were clinging to a late lead, Meredith also fumbled a quick tap directly in front of Japan's posts that handed back possession.
"He (Kiss) has given us the licence to play and is all for us having a crack … (If we) see space, take it," Lonergan said.
"Now it's up to us to know how to make use of it … we've got to know that when we take that space we've got to be on board with it and commit."
Kiss made four changes to the starting side in his first Test since taking over from Schmidt, and said more were inevitable in Townsville.
Lock Miles Amatosero's red card will likely see him suspended — Lukhan Salakaia-Loto was firming for a Townsville return anyway — while the forward pack could be shuffled further, with prop Isaac Kailea unused in Japan after his recall to the squad.
Called in as injury cover, young prop Massimo De Lutiis could become Wallaby number 1,000 in Townsville.
How Kiss responds to Meredith's middling night, particularly with the boot in general play, will be crucial given Carter Gordon is fit for a return.
"I'd imagine there will be one or two tweaks," Kiss said.
"This trip took a lot out of a lot of guys; we'll have to look at where the energy is, and at the numbers, because you lose a lot of weight."
Kiss acknowledged his side's kicking struggles, that they had overplayed or been impatient and struggled to defend around the ruck.
But the coach's composure in the box rubbed off, cameras catching him laughing late in the game with the result in the balance.
"It wasn't frivolous," Kiss said of the coaching booth gag.
"We're learning about each other in the box, getting on well, and it was a nice little moment; I know it was tight, but you've got to have fun doing this, too."
Kiss, the former Kangaroos and Queensland rugby league winger, has pushed an Australia brand to his playing style, likening the mentality to backyard footy and cricket battles.
"It's a balance of backing yourself and managing the game at the right time," he said.
"I just ask us to be skilful, and the nature of the game at times probably said 'let's just hold it a bit'.
"We had to come up with quality when shit happened, and we did that."
