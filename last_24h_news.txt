Bar Association broadly backs new offence for persistent child sexual abuse
Hong Kong Bar Association vice-chairman Martin Hui says four ‘safeguards’ are needed to ensure fairness for both defendant and victim
Vice-chairman Martin Hui Siu-ting said that while the association had reversed previous reservations it expressed when the city last discussed such a proposal in 2001, the new offence would require safeguards to ensure both sides were treated fairly at trial.
“We broadly agree to the introduction of such an offence of persistent child sexual abuse, which somehow reflects the past experience of at least 20 years of different jurisdictions within the criminal system,” Hui said.
Authorities previously attempted to introduce such an offence between 2001 and 2002 to allow prosecutors to lay charges for long-term, repetitive sexual abuse even when the child concerned could not accurately recall the exact circumstances of each incident.

Chinese scientists find some Yangtze flood controls may do more harm than good
By compressing rivers and stripping away natural defences, human engineering may be worsening flood impacts, prompting a global rethink
But a new study has uncovered a paradox: some human interventions may have inadvertently worsened the very flood risks they were built to mitigate.
“We show that flood frequency peaked during the Little Ice Age [1500−1850] … and that human interventions, particularly embankments and lake reclamation, amplified climate-driven flood magnitudes,” a group of scientists in China and Europe wrote in a paper published on Monday in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America.
They found that while climate remains the primary driver of flood occurrence, human interventions on the Yangtze River actually intensified climate-induced flood risks, rather than mitigating them.
Their analysis showed that constructing embankments and lake enclosures for agricultural use amplified the magnitude of 100-year floods, which have a 1 per cent chance of happening in any year, by 18 and 8 per cent respectively.
