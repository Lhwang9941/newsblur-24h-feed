Trump takes latest swipe at Canada, suggesting the US should build a border wall to keep smoke out
An AI slop image of a giant air filter separating the countries includes a sign with the slogan ‘Clean Air. Clear Borders’
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
President Donald Trump followed up his repeated threats to impose tariffs over Canadian wildfire smoke with a blast of social media hot air Saturday.
Trump trolled America's northern neighbor by posting an AI slop image of a giant pleated air filter along the U.S. border with Canada.
A sign at the base of the imaginary structure — reminiscent of the still-unfinished border wall with Mexico that Trump made a cornerstone of his winning 2016 campaign — calls it the "North America Air Filter Barrier" and carries the slogan, "Clean Air. Clear Borders."
But in an apparent AI instruction error, the image shows bright blue skies and puffy white clouds on both sides of the massive air purifier.
A spokesperson for Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney didn't immediately return an inquiry from The Independent.
On Monday, Trump imposed 50 percent tariffs on a range of Canadian products over what he called Canada's "discrimination" against U.S. commerce.
He’s also threatened additional tariffs over the wildfire smoke that drifted from Canada into the U.S. earlier this month, causing unhealthy air in cities including Detroit, New York and Washington, D.C.
The latest threat came Friday, when he told reporters in the Oval Office, "We're going to put a big tariff on Canada because of the smoke," according to the CBC/Radio-Canada.
The U.S. Supreme Court in February overruled earlier tariffs that Trump imposed on Canada.
Trump has been picking fights with Canada since shortly after winning a second term in 2024, suggesting the U.S. would annex the Great White North and turn it into the 51st state.
He mockingly referred to then-Prime Minister Justin Trudeau as the "Governor of the Great State of Canada" and has likewise called Trudeau’s successor Carney the "future Governor of Canada."
Canadians responded to the mistreatment with a "Buy Canadian" boycott that resulted in a 21.5 percent drop in vacation travel to the U.S. last year, Canada's Global News website reported Wednesday.
Canadian provinces also barred government-run liquor stores from selling American spirits, resulting in a nearly 70 percent plunge in U.S. exports of Kentucky bourbon and other booze to what was once American distillers' second-largest market, Fox News reported in March.
A recent Pew Research Center poll also found that 79 percent of Canadians have no confidence in Trump’s leadership, despite a recent Gallup survey that showed 80 percent of Americans held a positive view of Canada.

The police arrested nine protesters Saturday night after establishing that the protest was unlawful because of a lack of coordination with the security forces.
The protest, which was held on Kaplan Street in Tel Aviv, was evacuated shortly after, the police said in a statement.
"During a protest that took place without authorization and without coordination with the police this evening on Kaplan Street in Tel Aviv, several demonstrators began to disrupt public order and block the roadway, obstructing the movement of vehicles," the police said in a statement.
Protest groups demand October 7 inquiry
Earlier, several protest groups announced a mass demonstration in Habima Square in Tel Aviv on Saturday night, calling for the establishment of a state commission of inquiry into the October 7 massacre and demanding equality in bearing the burden of the war.
The protest was held three days before the Supreme Court is due to hear petitions against the law freezing the arrests of draft evaders, in a hearing set for Tuesday before an expanded panel of nine justices.
"The Knesset may have dissolved, but the government has not stopped trying to evade responsibility and place a heavier burden on the serving public," the protest organizers said.

Major fire destroys main building at Chabad founder's grave in Ukraine

A massive fire broke out shortly before midnight on Friday near the grave of Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi, the founder of the Chabad Hasidic movement, in Hadiach, in Ukraine’s Poltava region.
The complex, which includes the main synagogue, sustained millions of dollars in damage and was largely destroyed. Initial assessments indicated that the fire may have been caused by an electrical short circuit, although investigators are still examining all possible causes.
The kitchen and guest facilities were also damaged. No injuries were reported, and the local Chabad emissary, Rabbi Shneur Zalman Deitsch, and his family are safe. No further details were immediately available.
Major prayer site amid the war
The gravesite complex is the second largest Jewish prayer site in Ukraine. Before the war, tens of thousands of people visited the site each year. Despite the ongoing war, it continues to attract approximately 10,000 visitors annually.
About one third of those visitors arrive for the commemoration of the rabbi’s death during the Hebrew month of Tevet, which usually falls in December or January.
Other visitors include local Jews traveling in organized groups, as well as tourists from Israel, Europe, and the US. Many fly to Chisinau, Moldova, and drive 12- to 15-hours to Hadiach.
The Ba'al HaTanya’s flight and death
Rabbi Shneur Zalman, also known as the Ba'al HaTanya, was born in Liozna, in what is now Belarus, in 1745 and founded the Chabad Hasidic movement.
In 1812, Rabbi Shneur Zalman fled his home in Liadi during the war between Russia and France.
He became ill while fleeing toward Kremenchuk, in what is now Ukraine, and died on December 26, 1812.
His body was transported by wagon approximately 200 kilometers to Hadiach, where he was buried. Jews continued to visit his grave over the years, but the city’s Jewish population was murdered during the Holocaust. Communist rule later made it difficult for worshipers to reach the site.
In the early 1990s, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson sent special emissaries to restore the grave. The site has since become a major Jewish pilgrimage destination.
The scale of the complex
The complex was established nearly 20 years ago and continued to expand over the years. It included a large synagogue, which was completely destroyed in the fire, a ritual bath, dozens of guest rooms, a kitchen, and dining halls. Some of the other facilities were also damaged.
Several hundred Jews live in the Poltava region. Rabbi Shneur Zalman Deitsch, the rabbi of Hadiach and the official responsible for the complex, was not at the site when the fire broke out.
