EA Sports FC 27 details major gameplay updates, from AI to set pieces
EA Sports has revealed a first look at FC 27 gameplay and of its new open world mode The Grounds.
- Bookmark
As the countdown continues to the release of EA Sports FC 27, developers have shared a first look at what to expect from its gameplay, including a deep dive into some of the key mechanics coming to the latest instalment in EA’s iconic football franchise.
Kylian Mbappe was revealed as this year’s cover star across the Standard and Ultimate Editions, with his Real Madrid teammate, and one of last year’s cover stars, Jude Bellingham joining him on the Ultimate Plus Edition cover.
Launching in September, developers have shared a first look at what to expect in FC 27, starting with gameplay.

‘EA Sports FC 27’ gameplay
Developers have said they have continued to take player feedback on board, focusing on more intelligent attacking options such as different run types, fundamental changes to AI defending with more responsive and fluid animations and refreshed mechanics for corners and crosses, given how popular set pieces have become in football.
Through balls have been tweaked so they’re less assisted, meaning players are less prone to deadly counter-attacks. Trigger runs are being altered too.
Attacking spatial awareness is a big focus in FC 27, especially with contextual runs that will now be much more decisive. That means players will attack space more instead of holding back and sticking to their formation position without needing to trigger this.
Pass and follow can be triggered by passing the ball while holding the top left and right bumpers. This means players will pass the ball and then move into space to either tee up a one-two or exploit space themselves.
Curved runs can also be triggered while holding the same two buttons when in possession. Players will need to keep hold of the ball for longer until the passing lane opens, but this should allow for improved chance creation.
Offside awareness for attackers has also been improved, meaning when a player tries to beat the defensive line, they hold, check or curve their run slightly if required instead of just running in behind as fast as possible.
On the defensive side of things, auto-tackles from the AI have been reduced. They are more likely to hold attackers up to stop them being bypassed easily. The distance players keep while jockeying attackers has been reduced. That actually works in favour of the attacking team, especially with quick-footed players who can get shots off more quickly.
However, the range of manual tackling has improved in favour of the defence, meaning players are more likely to win the ball in close proximity. When defending and intercepting, the ball is less likely to bounce off players and back to the attacking team, as defenders are more likely to retain possession when cutting off passing lanes.
Improved animations should result in better passing fluidity as well, with defenders not getting turned as often and increased jockey speed.
Crosses and corners have been given a big revamp, with two-step dynamic corners, crossing into space and deep free-kick movement. With corners, after the kick has been powered up, players can now switch to a player in the box to meet the delivery manually. Deep free-kicks act in a similar way.
Crosses are now much more dependent on manual inputs in terms of aim and power. They are also more likely to be aimed into space for attacking players to run onto.
Off-balance dribbling has been improved as well. Inspired by Lionel Messi, when defenders get close to pushing attackers off balance, they can still continue their run with the ball if the tackle is not completed. It allows for much better control in tight spaces. A dribbling rating of 85 or more is required to do this.
Authentic gameplay has been revamped too. It was introduced in FC 26 for single-player modes, including Career, to provide a more realistic experience on the pitch and has been enhanced further in FC 27.
It will feel more grounded, opponents will be smarter and less predictable, movement will be more realistic in different situations and there are more customisation options, too.
Generally, one-on-ones have been balanced more in favour of the attacker, through-ball aiming is now much less forgiving and playstyles have been rebalanced, too. New skill moves have been added as well, including stepover combos, fake turn, ball roll spin and kneel header.

Frasier star won role ‘in really weird way’ after Kelsey Grammer hesitation
Sitcom’s lead star wasn’t initially sure about casting decision
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
Kelsey Grammer exemplified the pickiness of his sitcom character Frasier Crane by almost blocking his co-star from being cast in the series.
Jane Leeves, 65, played Mancunian live-in caregiver Daphne Moon on the hit Cheers spin-off, but she very nearly lost the role because Grammer didn’t want the character to be British.
The actor, who hails from Ilford, Essex, made it through to auditions, and when Grammer heard her accent, he was concerned it would be too similar to Mary Poppins-lite series Nanny and the Professor, which aired in the 1970s.
“I got Frasier in a really weird way,” Leeves recalled in a new interview with Still Hot in Cleveland podcast.
“My agent said to me, ‘There’s some interest in you for this spinoff from Cheers.’ And I loved Cheers. I went in to read for the producers. Cynthia Nixon was in the room, she went in before me. I went in, I read for the producers.
“They said, ‘Hold on a second. Could you just wait outside?’ And I went outside, then Jeff Greenberg, the casting director, came out and he said, ‘Would you be able to come in tomorrow to read with Kelsey?’”
The following day, Leeves returned – but things appeared to go suddenly south when Grammer arrived. “He marched in and they called me in and we were about to go in the room to read with the producers and Kelsey went, ‘Nope.’ Told the producer to stay outside, because he didn’t want an English girl. He thought it would be Nanny and the Professor.”
However, the producers insisted that Leeves read with Grammer, 71, who was won over by her audition.
“I was sitting outside and then Kelsey came out, ran off, jumped in his Corvette, and they called me back into the room and they said, ‘Welcome to the show.’” The Independent has contacted Grammer for comment.
Frasier – a spin-off focused on his Cheers character – originally aired for 11 seasons between 1993 and 2004, and won 37 Primetime Emmys.
A reboot premiered in 2023 and returned for a second season the following year before getting axed by Paramount+. It received generally lacklustre reviews from critics.
David Hyde Pierce, who played Frasier’s younger brother Niles in the series, did not return for the revival, much to the disappointment of Frasier fans.
“David basically decided he wasn’t really interested in repeating the performance of Niles,” Grammer said at the time.
In an interview with the Los Angeles Times, Hyde Pierce explained: “It’s not like I said, ‘Oh, I don’t ever want to do that again.’ I loved every moment. It was that I wanted to do other things.”
Hyde Pierce said that other work commitments, including the US series Julia, confirmed his decision, stating: “When we got into real talks about the reboot, I had just started on Julia and was working on a musical and going to do another musical. I just thought, ‘I don’t want to be committed to a show and not be able to do stuff like this.’”
He also said he thought “they don’t actually need me” as “Frasier has moved on to a new world”.
“They have new characters. And I think I’m right. It’s doing great. And the new people they have are great.”

YUZHNO-SAKHALINSK, July 30. /TASS/. Gazprom is currently building an LNG plant as part of the ethane-containing gas processing facility in Ust-Luga based on its own large-scale gas liquefaction technology, Deputy Chairman Vitaly Markelov told a forum.
"As of today, the project I mentioned, the LNG plant in Ust-Luga, is already being implemented with our Gazprom DMR license and the supply of domestically produced equipment," he said.
"Gazprom is constructing an LNG plant based on the Russian large-scale Gazprom DMR liquefaction technology with the use of domestic equipment developed through R&D," according to the presentation for Markelov’s address.
The project to build a gas processing and liquefaction facility is being implemented by Gazprom and RusGazDobycha (with RusKhimAlyans owned equally by Gazprom and RusGazDobycha acting as operator). The project entails the creation of an integrated natural gas processing and liquefaction complex near the Ust-Luga seaport in the Leningrad Region. Gas from the Nadym-Pur-Tazovsky region will serve as feedstock. As of the end of June 2026, the Ust-Luga complex was 80% complete.
The facility will annually process 45 bln cubic meters of gas and produce 13.1 mln tons of liquefied natural gas, up to 3.6 mln tons of ethane fraction, up to 1.7 mln tons of liquefied petroleum gases, and 0.13 mln tons of pentane-hexane fraction. Natural gas remaining after processing (18.9 bln cubic meters) will be directed into Gazprom’s gas transmission system. The gas-chemical plant will process ethane supplied by the complex and produce up to 2.8 mln tons of various grades of polyethylene.
The launch of the first train of the gas processing complex in Ust-Luga is scheduled for 2026, with the second train planned to be launched in 2027. The start-up of the first and second trains of the LNG plant is planned for 2027 and 2028, respectively. The gas-to-chemicals complex within the project will be launched in two stages, in 2026 and 2027.
