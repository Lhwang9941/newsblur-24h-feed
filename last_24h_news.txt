Drone enters Bulgaria, explodes near pipeline at Romanian border: Bulgarian PM
- A drone carrying a significant amount of explosives exploded near the Bulgarian-Romanian border, close to the Trans-Balkan gas pipeline compressor station, causing no casualties.
- Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev said the drone was large, not a hobby drone, and it crashed in a sunflower field after the explosion.
- The drone was not detected by either Bulgaria or Romania, highlighting the difficulty in identifying and neutralising such threats amid rising drone incidents in Eastern Europe linked to the Ukraine conflict.

SOFIA - A drone that entered Bulgaria on Aug 8 exploded near the Romanian border and the Trans-Balkan gas pipeline, which links Turkey to Ukraine, Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev announced.
Radev did not specify where the drone, which caused no casualties, came from and said the authorities did not yet know what type it was.
But he said the amount of explosives it was carrying was “significant”.
“The drone exploded in the immediate vicinity of the Kardam border crossing with Romania,” near the Black Sea in the country’s north-east, “1,000m from the Trans-Balkan gas pipeline’s compressor station,” he said in a video released by the government on social media.
The drone later crashed into a sunflower field, he said.
The sound of the drone was recorded by border police in Romania, and then “a loud explosion accompanied by black smoke” was spotted by a Bulgarian border police patrol, Radev added, speaking after an extraordinary meeting of his Cabinet’s security council.
“One thing is certain: the drone was carrying a significant amount of explosives, given that it could be heard from a distance and produced such black smoke,” he said.
The drone “crashed into a sunflower field, and I hope we will soon discover what type it is,” Radev said, adding that it was not a “hobby drone” but a “larger drone”.
He noted that the drone had not been detected by either of the two neighbouring countries.
“As you know, identifying and neutralising these drones poses a major challenge,” he added.
Incidents involving drones have become increasingly frequent in recent months across eastern European countries that are NATO members and supporters of Ukraine in its conflict with Russia.
Romania, which has faced frequent drone incursions – including some crashes on its territory – since the start of Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, ordered the expulsion of a Russian embassy staff member in late July. AFP

Pasta salads sold at Asda and Morrisons recalled over listeria fears
The products have been recalled due to a possible contamination of the infection that in severe cases can cause meningitis
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
A number of supermarket pasta products have been recalled due to a possible listeria contamination.
Food producer Greencore has recalled three pasta products because of possible contamination with Listeria monocytogenes, the Food Standards Agency said.
They are Asda cheese and tomato pasta salad; Asda nacho cheese pasta salad; and Morrisons chicken and bacon pasta.
Anyone who has bought the products is advised not to eat them. Instead, customers are advised to return them to their nearest store where they will be given a full refund. You do not need your receipt.
The recall notice says: “Greencore is recalling the above products. Point of sale notices will be displayed in all retail stores that are selling this product.
“These notices explain to customers why the products are being recalled and tell them what to do if they have bought the products.”
Listeriosis symptoms
The NHS says that listeriosis is a rare infection, which is caused by bacteria called listeria. It usually goes away on its own, but it can make some people seriously ill.
Listeriosis is usually caused by eating food contaminated with listeria bacteria.
Listeria can contaminate a wide range of foods, but most infections are caused by eating chilled, ready-to-eat foods, such as:
- cold, cooked sliced meats and cured meats
- smoked and cured fish – including in sushi
- cooked shellfish
- soft mould-ripened cheeses (such as camembert and brie) and blue-veined cheeses
- pâté
- pre-prepared sandwiches and salads
- pre-cut fruit (such as pre-packed melon slices)
- unpasteurised milk
- dairy products made from unpasteurised milk
Symptoms caused by Listeria monocytogenes can be similar to flu and include high temperature, muscle ache or pain, chills, feeling or being sick and diarrhoea. However, in rare cases, the infection can be more severe, causing serious complications, such as meningitis, the Food Standards Agency said.
Some people are more vulnerable to listeria infections, including those over 65 years of age, pregnant women and their unborn babies, babies less than one month old and people with weakened immune systems.

STOCKHOLM, August 8. /TASS/. The Swedish authorities continue to indulge the Kiev regime, including by violating existing norms of international maritime law, the Russian Embassy in the kingdom told TASS regarding the Swedish Supreme Court's refusal to review the case of the transfer to Ukraine of the Caffa dry cargo ship detained by the Swedish Coast Guard.
"We consider this decision politically motivated. We note that the Swedish authorities continue to indulge the Kiev regime, including by violating existing norms of international maritime law. As we see in numerous examples, they do not stop short of outright lawlessness and actions whose justification is untenable," the Russian diplomatic mission noted.
On August 5, the Swedish Supreme Court refused to review the decisions of the district and appellate courts that the Caffa dry cargo ship could be transferred to Ukraine. "The Supreme Court has examined the materials and found no grounds for granting leave to appeal," the court documents noted.
The Ystad District Court in June decided that the Caffa, which was detained by Swedish authorities in March and is located in the port of Trelleborg, could be transferred to Ukraine. The Court of Appeal upheld this verdict in July. The Ukrainian prosecutor's office sent a request to Sweden for the handover of the vessel to Ukraine on the grounds that it had previously sailed under the Russian flag and had been used in the export of grain.
The vessel was detained by Swedish police and the coast guard on suspicion of sailing under false documents and lacking state registration. The captain, a Russian citizen, was taken into custody and released at the end of April. The majority of the crew members are also Russian citizens; they left the kingdom on May 12.
