Holding by-election to replace disgraced lawmaker low priority, John Lee says
Weighing in on the issue for first time, city leader stresses electoral authorities need to consider ‘necessity of poll’
Holding a by-election to fill a seat vacated by former lawmaker William Wong Kam-fai after he resigned over a drink-driving arrest is a lower priority than preparing three important polls over the next 18 months, Hong Kong’s leader has said.
Weighing in on the issue for the first time nearly a month after Wong’s resignation, Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu stressed that electoral authorities needed to weigh the “necessity of a poll”, alongside manpower and logistics in deciding whether to trigger an election.
Lee on Tuesday gave the clearest signal so far among officials that the vote to replace the resigned lawmaker would not take place, after Secretary for Constitutional and Mainland Affairs Janice Tse Siu-wa cited time constraints and concerns about the cost-effectiveness of holding such a poll.
“When we look at the three important elections we have in the next 18 months, our priority must be successfully conducting these three polls rather than the by-election for the vacant Legco seat, in terms of time and workload,” Lee said.
The three scheduled polls are the Election Committee subsector election this November, the chief executive poll next March and the District Council election towards the end of next year.
When electoral authorities weigh the “necessity of a poll”, factors such as manpower, venue arrangements and the procurement of election materials must be considered “based on the actual situation”, Lee said.

France firestorm phenomenon: monster clouds that spawn lightning and new blazes
The inferno ravaging southwest France creates rare fire clouds, never before recorded in the country
A wildfire in southwest France grew so powerful that its smoke column became a thunderstorm, generating lightning that struck the ground and ignited new fires beyond the original blaze, French officials said.
Put another way, that means the inferno was no longer simply being propelled by the weather. It was making its own. The thundercloud also produced violent, erratic winds capable of driving the flames in new directions.
The phenomenon, called a pyrocumulonimbus, or pyroCb, is rare in Europe and more commonly documented in North America and Australia, where it occurs only over some of the most extreme fires. France’s national firefighters federation said it had never before recorded one in the country.
The blaze erupted near Saumos on July 22 and has since burned more than 420 sq km (162 square miles) of forests and scrubland, damaged or destroyed more than 240 homes and forced 220,000 people to evacuate in Gironde.
At about 6.20pm on Friday, two days after the fire began, the departmental fire and rescue service said it had generated a pyrocumulonimbus. The cloud weakened overnight as humidity rose, then formed again several times.
Repeatedly, heat and smoke have surged upwards into a vast black thundercloud, electrified and lit from within.
