Turkish police detain actor-turned-mayor Erdal Besikcioglu in latest raids targeting opposition
ISTANBUL (AP) — Police detained a former TV actor-turned-mayor on Thursday in the latest wave of arrests targeting the opposition.
Erdal Besikcioglu, the mayor of Etimesgut Municipality in western Ankara, is being investigated for allegations of irregularities and forgery in municipal tenders, the Justice Ministry said in a statement. Investigators are also examining parking lot leasing schemes and construction zoning permissions.
Besikcioglu played grizzled detective Behzat C in a popular police drama of the same name that ran from 2010 to 2019. He was elected mayor in 2024 with the opposition Republican People’s Party, or CHP, in an election where the opposition strengthened its grip on major cities, including Ankara and Istanbul.
Hundreds of elected mayors and other officials have been arrested in the past two years as part of criminal investigations, mostly into alleged corruption, in operations targeting opposition-run municipalities.
Ekrem Imamoglu, the mayor of Istanbul widely considered the strongest potential challenger to President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, has also been arrested and imprisoned since March last year. He faces criminal cases that could result in decades-long sentences.
Erdogan’s government insists that Turkey’s courts are impartial and act independently of political pressure.
Some 42 municipal officials and 13 company representatives were targeted in Thursday’s dawn raids across nine provinces overseen by the Ankara West Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office, the Justice Ministry said. Three deputy mayors were also arrested.
The suspects are being held on charges of establishing and running a criminal organization; membership in a criminal organization; embezzlement; bribery; extortion; and tender-rigging.
Police detained 52 suspects and conducted searches at 71 locations, including homes and offices. Documents and digital materials were seized during the raids, the ministry’s statement added.
In May, the leadership of the CHP was removed by court order, prompting the formation of the New Party by deposed party head Ozgur Ozel last Friday. Ninety CHP legislators quit the CHP for the New Party, making it the largest opposition group in parliament.

Bolivian prosecutors on Wednesday issued an arrest warrant for former President Evo Morales, citing his alleged role in protests demanding resignation of the country's president.
According to the warrant shared online, the prosecutors are investigating Morales not only for his alleged involvement in the demonstrations but also on charges including endangering transportation safety, forming a criminal organization, public incitement to commit crimes, and attacks against the security of public services.
"For all the reasons set forth above, and given that the presence of the accused is essential, as he is the alleged perpetrator of the reported criminal acts, the undersigned Prosecutor … hereby issues, orders, and mandates the execution of this Prosecutor's Order," says the arrest warrant issued in the city of Santa Cruz.
In a statement posted on US social media platform X, Morales dismissed the accusations, saying "they will not intimidate us," and arguing that the government was ignoring the country's "deep" economic and social crisis.
"Today, the government has issued a new arrest warrant against me, accusing me of alleged crimes of terrorism and armed uprising. They are trying to hold us responsible for the social protests in order to conceal the real crisis facing Bolivia: a deep economic and social crisis resulting from a model that has abandoned the people and protects corruption," Morales said.
"They will not intimidate us. We will continue fighting alongside the people, as we always have, with the conviction that Bolivia deserves a different path—one of dignity, sovereignty, social justice, and the restoration of hope for everyone," he added.
Between May and June, farmers from La Paz and workers affiliated with the Bolivian Workers' Center, backed by pro-Morales groups, held seven weeks of road blockades across western and central Bolivia, demanding President Paz's resignation.
The blockades caused shortages of food, fuel, and medical oxygen and contributed to at least 16 deaths, including 13 patients who could not be reached by emergency medical teams in time.

Russia struck cities across Ukraine with a mass wave of missiles and drones overnight into 30 July 2026, killing civilians, including children, and injuring dozens, according to Ukraine's Air Force, State Emergency Service, and local authorities. Rescuers dug through collapsed homes in Lviv, in the capital, and in a village near Kryvyi Rih, and one Russian cruise missile crossed the border into Poland. It was the first mass strike on Ukraine's western oblasts since March.
The scale of the attack
Ukraine's Air Force tracked 358 incoming weapons — 74 missiles and 284 drones of various types. Air defenses shot down or suppressed 320 of them, 55 missiles and 265 drones. The missiles included Tsirkon and Oniks anti-ship missiles, Iskander-M, S-400, and North Korea's KN-23 ballistics, and Kh-101 and Kalibr cruise missiles, alongside Shahed, Gerbera, Italmas, and decoy drones launched from four Russian regions.
The ballistic missiles remain the biggest problem for Ukraine. Of the nine Russia fired, air defenses stopped only one. Impacts landed at 20 locations, with debris falling on 13 more.
President Volodymyr Zelenskyy put the current nationwide toll at eight dead and dozens wounded in the capital and across ten oblasts. He tied the losses directly to the missing air defenses.
"Protection from the Russian missile threat is the most important task for saving our people's lives," he wrote. "Untimely help, delays in supplying anti-ballistic missiles — that is exactly this destruction, exactly these casualties that, unfortunately, we have today."
A family of six killed near Kryvyi Rih
The heaviest loss of life came in Radushne, a village in Kryvyi Rih district of Dnipropetrovsk Oblast. An Iskander-M ballistic missile hit a house where a large family lived. Six people died, three of them children, and eight were wounded, the head of the Kryvyi Rih Defense Council, Oleksandr Vilkul, reported.
The head of Dnipropetrovsk Oblast, Oleksandr Hanzha, named the children killed as a six-year-old girl and boys of 11 and 17. An 84-year-old woman was hospitalized in serious condition, and a 49-year-old woman and boys of 6 and 15 were taken to hospital in moderate condition. Two houses were destroyed and five damaged. Rescuers pulled two children from the rubble alive.
Lviv: homes, a school, two kindergartens
Lviv neighborhoods took some of the worst damage. The number of wounded recently rose to 26, with 15 hospitalized, children among them, the head of Lviv Oblast, Maksym Kozytskyi, reported. One child was in the hospital.
Two residential buildings on Patona and Vyhovskoho streets were hit, and more than 20 apartment buildings were damaged, along with lyceum school No. 15 — where 140 windows and four doors were blown out — and two kindergartens. Mayor Andrii Sadovyi described a direct hit and no military target anywhere near it.
"Here on Patona we see a direct missile hit. The floors have essentially been sheared off," he said. "They simply attack residential buildings, kill people directly. There are no military targets here, nothing at all."
Rescuers freed a woman, a man, and his six-year-old son from a collapsed building, the Lviv Oblast prosecutor's office said. A crane was used to lift people from a damaged five-story block through a blocked stairwell.
One killed in Kyiv
In the capital, one person died and two were hurt, the Kyiv Military Administration and the Emergency Service reported. Fires broke out in two districts: market pavilions in Obolon and a garage cooperative in Sviatoshyn, where firefighters found a body while putting out the flames and hospitalized an injured man, the emergency service said.
In Skybyn, in Brovary district of Kyiv Oblast, a strike partly destroyed a two-story house and wounded five people, including a 16-year-old boy, the emergency service confirmed.
Poltava, Zhytomyr, Mykolaiv, and the coast
In Poltava district, warehouses caught fire from the strikes and one person died, the head of Poltava Oblast, Vitalii Diakivnych, said. A Nova Poshta parcel terminal was also hit, with no casualties there.
Mykolaiv Oblast came under a night of drone attacks that damaged port infrastructure, warehouses, homes, an ambulance, and an administrative building, but wounded no one, the oblast administration reported.
In Kharkiv, a jet-powered Shahed hit an industrial zone, Mayor Ihor Terekhov reported.
The Russian strikes injured three children and two adults in Zhytomyr Oblast, local authorities said.
Air defenses also worked over Kirovohrad Oblast, where a private yard was damaged in Svitlovodsk and no one was hurt, the oblast administration said.

A cruise missile in Poland
One Russian cruise missile crossed into Poland during the attack and came down in Lublin voivodeship, near the village of Targowisko, where a blast woke residents and rescuers found a crater in a field, the outlet lublin112 reported. Polish forces had put military aircraft into the air hours earlier because of Russia's long-range strikes on Ukraine, the country's Operational Command said, with fighters and an early-warning plane airborne and ground air defenses at readiness.
It was not the first such incursion. In 2023 a Russian Kh-55 flew several hundred kilometers over Polish territory before it fell near the city of Bydgoszcz. Earlier in July, the Polish outlet Onet, citing intelligence and diplomatic sources, warned that Poland could become a target of Russian provocations meant to test NATO's resolve.
