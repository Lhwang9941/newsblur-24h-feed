Police share details after 24-year-old shooter left three dead and more injured at an Idaho In-N-Out
The mass shooting left three dead and seven others injured, police said. In-N-Out confirmed that one of the deceased was a restaurant worker
- Bookmark
Police have released new details about a mass shooting at an Idaho In-N-Out on Saturday, a burst of violence that rattled the small city of Twin Falls.
The lone shooter died of a self-inflicted gunshot wound, Twin Falls Police Chief Matthew Hicks said at a Sunday afternoon news conference.
Three people were killed in the shooting, and seven others were wounded. Authorities have not released the names of the victims, though In-N-Out said one of the dead was an employee at the chain.
Hicks said the investigation remains ongoing and that authorities are still working to piece together “possible motivations.” The FBI, which is assisting the case, has opened a tip portal.
“Even the smallest detail may be important to our investigation,” the police chief said.
How did the violence unfold?
Hicks walked through the timeline of the shooting and its aftermath, which he described as “one of the most chaotic instances of what law enforcement officers encounter in their career.”
At 2:29 p.m. local time Saturday, 911 dispatchers received multiple calls reporting shots fired near the Twin Falls In-N-Out, located on Blue Lakes Boulevard North. “Dispatchers could hear screaming,” Hicks said.
Officers rushed to the scene and “converged” on the restaurant, where unverified social media videos appeared to show the gunman firing a rifle in the parking lot and people running away from the area.
What is known about the gunman?
Investigators have named the gunman as 24-year-old Chad Williams, but did not offer any further information on him.
Police said that Williams died from a self-inflicted gunshot wound.
Hicks also condemned misinformation circulating on social media about the shooter’s identity, saying that, to the best of his knowledge, Williams acted alone.
“At this time, we are still working to determine the full set of circumstances in this incident, including the suspect's actions and possible motivations. The Williams family has been notified, and they are fully cooperating with law enforcement.
“Their hearts are also broken during this time.”
Who are the victims?
Police did not release the names of the three people killed or the seven wounded on Sunday. Earlier Saturday, a city spokesman told The Independent that three people had died and two had been wounded, while stressing that the toll was likely to rise.
Of the injured, two have been released from the hospital, three are in stable condition and two remain in critical condition.
Hicks acknowledged the toll the shooting has taken on Twin Falls, a community of about 56,000 people, located roughly 130 miles southeast of Boise.
“Together our community will get through this,” he said. “Remain Twin Falls strong, and support each other.”
One witness earlier described seeing a woman in an In-N-Out uniform bleeding from a gunshot wound to the chest. Another worker dragged her across the parking lot, where paramedics later treated her.
“She was pretty bad off, but I don't know. I hope she made it,” the witness said.
In a separate statement Saturday, Lynsi Snyder, president of In-N-Out, confirmed that one of the restaurant’s associates had died.
“We lost one of our beautiful Associates tonight,” she wrote in a social media post. “She was taking care of our most important asset - our Customers, and her life was taken by a disgusting human who doesn’t value the lives of others or his own.”
Off-duty cop and passerby hailed as heroes
Police say that an off-duty state trooper and passerby engaged the gunman as the violence unfolded and prevented even greater loss of life.
“We believe their actions helped drive the suspect away from the scene, preventing further casualties,” Hall told reporters.
Later in the Sunday briefing, Robert Bohls, special agent in charge of the FBI’s Salt Lake City office, said the bureau is “working tirelessly to support the investigation.”
He urged anyone with information to contact the agency through its tip portal, where the public can submit photos and videos related to the shooting.
Idaho’s governor and representatives in Congress said on Saturday that they were monitoring the situation and keeping the Twin Falls community in their prayers.
So far in 2026, there have been 332 mass shootings in the U.S., according to the Mass Shooter Tracker, which defines mass shootings as instances in which four or more people are shot.

Cronulla coach's belief grows as Sharks surge towards NRL finals

Cronulla coach Craig Fitzgibbon says he believes in his team more than they believe in themselves, as the Sharks hit their stride close to finals.
The Sharks are the form team of the NRL, with nine wins from their last 10 games to sit in fourth spot.
What's next?
The Sharks will take on St George Illawarra on Sunday.
Cronulla is promising no let-up after extending its league-best winning streak to five matches, sparking talk that an elusive second NRL premiership is in the offing for the surging Sharks.
Rarely has understated coach Craig Fitzgibbon been more bullish about his belief in the team than after Sunday's dazzling 32-16 dispatch of South Sydney.
"I've never felt any different about the group in the last five years, to be honest, but maybe I believe in them more than they believe in themselves," he said.
With Nicho Hynes back to the vintage form that earned him the Dally M Medal and the Sharks a top-two finish in 2022, and his halves partner, Braydon Trindall, in career-best touch, the Sharks are flying with nine wins from their past 10 outings.
Michael Ennis, the club's 2016 grand final-winning hooker-turned-analyst, believes the Sharks will go all the way again a decade on from breaking the longest and most infamous title drought in premiership history.
"We're working hard towards something and we've been building that for a little bit," Fitzgibbon said.
"And then sometimes you get things go your way, sometimes they don't. But at the moment, it's going our way.
"There's still plenty more. We'll have a hiccup here and there and there'll be turbulence, but you won't challenge me on how much I believe in them."
Sitting fourth and certain not to face any more top-eight opposition until the finals, the Sharks are well placed to snatch an all-important top-two spot.
But skipper Blayke Brailey says the Sharks won't be taking their foot off the pedal, with the side's immediate focus on Sunday's derby with St George Illawarra.
The Dragons may be wooden spoon-bound but the Sharks won't be taking their arch rivals lightly, especially with points differential between the top teams looking increasingly important.
"A derby clash, no matter where you're sitting on the table, it's a tough contest," Brailey said.
"Those derby games are tough, no matter the form you're in."
After the Dragons at Kogarah, the Sharks face Canberra at home, the Titans on the Gold Coast, Parramatta away and Melbourne in a potentially crucial last-round battle at Shark Park.

Briton survives after two killed in collision between Greek firefighting helicopters
A Greek and a Danish national were killed in the tragedy as firefighters battled huge flames in Attica, near Athens
- Bookmark
- CommentsGo to comments
A Briton has survived and two people were killed after two firefighting helicopters collided while battling vast blazes tearing through an area west of Athens on Sunday.
A Greek and a Danish national on one helicopter were killed, after a second helicopter with a Briton and Greek onboard collided with its rotors. Both people onboard the second helicopter survived.
Tragic footage of the collision shows two helicopters crossing paths at low altitude, before the main rotor of the lower helicopter strikes the underside of the other, causing it to crash down in flames. The second helicopter was able to fly away and carry out an emergency landing.
Both the Greek prime minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis and president Konstantinos Tasoulas have paid tribute to firefighters killed as they were fighting a wildfire that has destroyed more than 100 homes around Psatha, Attica, northwest of Athens.
Europe has been ravaged by wildfires this summer after a period of record-breaking heatwaves and little rain – conditions that scientists say have been exacerbated by climate change.
Destructive blazes in France and Spain showed some signs of easing over the weekend, but several wildfires have broken out in Greece after a period of relative calm there.
Earlier this week, two firefighters died in Crete after becoming trapped between two fire fronts in the region of Rethymno on Wednesday. A third firefighter was found unconscious near the southern Peloponnese port town of Gytheio and was later pronounced dead.
Investigations into the helicopter crash are now underway, and no theories have yet been shared.
Aeronautical engineer Phaedon Karaiosifidis told Greek outlet Proto Thema that the factors which will be looked into are human error, mechanical failure, and weather conditions.
The outlet said that sources from the company that had leased the two helicopters reported that initial indications suggested human error was the cause of the collision, and that there were no reports of technical failure.
Greek prime minister Mr Mitsotakis has expressed his “deepest sorrow” over the deaths of a Danish pilot and Greek coordinator.
“The loss of the Greek coordinator and the Danish pilot while they were operating in the major fire at Porto Germeno fills us all with grief. Sincere condolences to their families,” he said.
Greek president Mr Tasoulas, who visited the Civil Protection Coordination Center in Athens on Sunday, said the “tragic incident” highlights the “dangerousness of the challenges faced by Fire Department Personnel”.
EU Commission president Ursula von der Leyen also offered her condolences, adding: “It takes a special courage to fly towards the flames so that others can be safe.
“As we continue to battle these fires side by side, Europe grieves with Greece and Denmark.”
Authorities are still looking into the cause of the fire in Attica. Early investigations suggest it started in the region of Boeotia and was caused by sparks from conductors on a private network transmitting electricity generated by wind turbines to the main power grid, the Kathimerini website reported.
Two Greek nationals were arrested on suspicion of negligence, while another suspect remains at large, the report said.
Gale-force winds that had fuelled another fire around Porto Germeno on the Gulf of Corinth 40 miles northwest of Athens subsided, but flames crossed a mountain to the south and reached the settlement of Veniza and a military firing range, igniting unexploded ordnance.
Emergency services focussed on preventing the fire from advancing towards the coastal town of Megara, which hosts a population of around 30,000.
"There are moments when the force of nature and the intensity of weather conditions exceed all human planning and every operational capability,” Mr Mitsotakis said in his regular Sunday message.
Greek authorities issued emergency alerts for evacuations for a number of settlements in western Attica, including an industrial estate near Megara.
In the village of Agios Konstantinos, residents were doing their best to halt the spread of the blaze.
"The fire has been burning since last night, it is certain that they cannot have forces everywhere. We as residents are doing everything we can,” said Tasos Tzempelikos, 61, who along with his son was pouring water on the fire.
Overnight, several settlements on the Ionian island of Kefalonia were evacuated after a fire broke out in the Pastra area in the south.
